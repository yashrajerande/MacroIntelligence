India Macro Regime

7.8% GDP Headline — Two-Wheeler Recovery vs Four-Wheeler Fade

The facts:

  • India GDP at 7.8% YoY (64th percentile) — but RBI's own forecast is 6.7% YoY, a 110bps gap that reflects the statistical discrepancy between headline output and ground-level demand.
  • Two-wheeler sales at 33.1% YoY signal rural recovery; but passenger vehicle sales fell for two consecutive months to 7.9% YoY and commercial vehicle sales to 12.6% YoY — urban and freight demand softening together.
  • PMI Composite at 55.9 index (85th percentile) with manufacturing at 55.1 and services at 55.2 — both accelerating — confirms the activity pulse is real.
  • But core sector growth decelerated to 4.8% YoY even as IIP capital goods hit 16.9% YoY — investment in machinery is outrunning infrastructure throughput.

The tension: The headline GDP and PMI signal a broad expansion; the vehicle sales decomposition and core sector deceleration signal a narrower, investment-led cycle with consumption bifurcating along the rural-urban axis.

Bottom line: Watch two-wheeler sales for rural demand confirmation — a third consecutive month above 25% YoY would validate the consumption broadening thesis; a reversal would not.

02
Global Macro Regime

US PMI at 91st Percentile — Growth Signal, Tightening Consequence

The facts:

  • US PMI Composite at 58.4 index (91st percentile, rising two consecutive months) and Global PMI Composite at 54.3 index (84th percentile) — the strongest synchronised DM growth signal since 2021.
  • But US GDP SAAR at 2.2% SAAR (50th percentile) lags the PMI optimism; US CPI at 3.72% YoY rising three consecutive months means the Fed has no permission to cut.
  • BOJ at 1.25% p.a. (100th percentile, rising two consecutive months) — the highest rate in the dataset — is unwinding the yen carry trade that funded EM inflows for three years.
  • US 10Y Treasury at 5.1% yield (92nd percentile) — down from 5.19% but structurally elevated — keeps the India-US yield differential compressed and EM carry unattractive.

The tension: DM PMIs are pricing re-acceleration; DM central banks are still tightening or holding at cycle highs. The bond market is not giving equities the rate relief they are pricing.

Bottom line: BOJ at 1.25% p.a. is the underappreciated tail — a further 25bps hike would trigger yen carry unwind and EM liquidity withdrawal within 60 days.

03
Liquidity Conditions

SIP Autopilot Masks NFO Collapse — Smart Money Pausing

The facts:

  • SIP inflows at ₹32,490 cr / month (93rd percentile), rising three consecutive months — the structural retail bid is intact and absorbing market volatility.
  • But NFO collections at ₹998 cr / month (2nd percentile, down from ₹3,051 cr / month) — the lowest in the dataset — signal that fund houses are not launching because they cannot deploy capital at current valuations.
  • DII equity net inflows at ₹51,829 cr / month (95th percentile) are barely offsetting FII equity net outflows of ₹49,935 cr / month (17th percentile) — a ₹1,894 cr / month net margin.
  • MF Average AUM at ₹87.09 lakh cr (86th percentile) — down from ₹88.31 lakh cr — reflects mark-to-market pressure despite inflow momentum.

The tension: Retail SIP flows are structurally resilient; institutional and fund-house behaviour (NFO freeze, FII exit) signals that the marginal buyer at current prices is the least informed one.

Bottom line: If FII outflows sustain above ₹40,000 cr / month for a second consecutive month, DII absorption will require SIP acceleration that the 10.7% YoY SIP growth rate cannot guarantee.

Equity + Real Estate Implications

NRI Bid Holds Premium Segment — REIT Prices Contradict Office Rents

The facts:

  • NRI buying is RISING — NRI share of residential purchases at 20% (up from 10%, +10 pp), with NRI share within premium/luxury at 25%; remittances at $73 bn (12.8% YoY) provide the funding base.
  • Hyderabad leads city momentum at 15% YoY sales growth with launches up 120% YoY — but 28 months of unsold inventory is the trapdoor beneath the headline.
  • Bengaluru office absorption at 5.2 mn sq ft / quarter (11% YoY) with rents at ₹109/sqft/mo — but Embassy REIT at ₹370/unit is down from ₹435, Mindspace at ₹345/unit down from ₹495.
  • Bank Nifty at 55,844 (92nd percentile) — flat on the session — while credit-deposit stress at 81% CD ratio should be compressing NIM expectations, not expanding them.

The tension: Office rents are rising and GCC demand (41% of occupier mix) is structurally sound, but REIT unit prices have fallen 15-30% — the market is pricing a vacancy risk that the rent data does not yet confirm.

Bottom line: REITs are a buy only if office vacancy stabilises below 14% — watch the next quarterly absorption print; above 14.5% vacant triggers further REIT derating.

Key Risks to Monitor

Four Risks, One Trigger — Crude Holds the Key

The facts:

  • Fuel inflation at 22.93% YoY (95th percentile) with Brent at $109.69/bbl — every $10 adds ~$15 bn to India's import bill, widens CAD by ~40bps, and pushes CPI toward 6.5% YoY by Q1 FY28.
  • BOJ at 1.25% p.a. (100th percentile) — a further 25bps hike unwinds yen carry, accelerates FII outflows beyond ₹49,935 cr / month, and tests RBI's FX intervention capacity.
  • CD ratio at 81% (73rd percentile) with credit growing at 19.4% YoY — if deposit growth slips below 15% YoY, NBFC funding costs rise and the bear scenario (credit crunch cascade) activates.
  • INR at 95.24 ₹/USD (89th percentile) with India VIX at 14.025 (9th percentile) — complacency in volatility pricing while currency stress is at a near-decade high is a classic pre-dislocation setup.

The tension: All four risks share a common accelerant: a Brent print above $115/bbl would simultaneously worsen CAD, force RBI to defend INR, tighten domestic liquidity, and compress corporate margins — the bear scenario needs only one catalyst.

Bottom line: Watch Brent crude this week — a close above $112/bbl shifts the base scenario to bear and makes an RBI emergency communication (not a cut) the most likely policy response.

NRI Bid Rising — Top End Supplied

The facts:

  • Affordable (< ₹40 lakh) took 16% of sales vs 14% of launches — supply and demand matched
  • City momentum: Hyderabad leads at 15% sales YoY; Chennai trails at -10%
  • NRI buying is RISING: 20% of residential purchases (+10 pp vs prior period in source); 25% within premium/luxury; concentrated in Mumbai Metropolitan Region, Bengaluru, Hyderabad
  • Office: Bengaluru leads leasing at 5.2 mn sq ft / quarter (11% YoY); GCCs take 41% of leasing

The tension: Supply is following demand into the same bands with a lag; the balance shifts one quarter after the sales mix does.

Bottom line: Track the launch share of the undersupplied bands next quarter — supply catching up is what turns a price story into an inventory story.
