India Macro Regime

7.8% GDP Headline — Two-Wheeler Surge vs Passenger Vehicle Fade

The facts:

  • India GDP at 7.8% YoY with PMI Composite at 55.9 — both manufacturing (55.1) and services (55.2) in expansion, capacity utilisation at a 3-year high of 77.4% of capacity.
  • IIP capital goods at 16.9% YoY confirms the investment cycle; core sector at 4.8% YoY (down from 5.0%) is the quiet dissent.
  • But passenger vehicle sales decelerated to 7.9% YoY from 26.1% two months prior — two consecutive months of decline — while 2-wheeler sales surged to 33.1% YoY, signalling a mass-market recovery that urban discretionary is not sharing.
  • GST at ₹2,03,521 cr / month (74th percentile) holds, but e-commerce GMV growth at 12.4% YoY (16th percentile, slowest in dataset) suggests digital consumption is not keeping pace with the headline.

The tension: GDP and PMI signal broad expansion; the consumption decomposition tells a different story — rural informal demand is recovering while urban formal consumption decelerates, a divergence that GST aggregates cannot hide.

Bottom line: Watch PV sales for a third consecutive monthly decline — if it prints below 5% YoY, the consumption-led growth narrative requires a significant downgrade.

02
Global Macro Regime

US Re-Acceleration Meets BOJ Tightening — EM Caught in the Crossfire

The facts:

  • US GDP SAAR accelerated to 3.4% SAAR (from 2.2%), US PMI Composite at 58.4 (80th percentile) — the strongest US growth signal in this cycle.
  • But US 10Y Treasury at 4.98% yield (90th percentile) and Fed Funds at 3.88% p.a. (rising 3 in a row) mean real tightening is already embedded in the curve.
  • BOJ rate at 1.25% p.a. — 10-year dataset high, 100th percentile — is the structural shift: yen carry unwind is not a tail risk, it is in progress.
  • Global PMI Composite at 54.3 (rising 2 in a row) and Eurozone GDP at 1.2% YoY (recovering from 0.6%) provide a floor, but China at 4.4% YoY with PMI at 52.1 is not the commodity demand engine it once was.

The tension: US growth is re-accelerating while the BOJ simultaneously tightens — the combination that historically forces EM carry unwinds is not a scenario, it is the current configuration.

Bottom line: BOJ's next move is the single most important variable for India liquidity flows; a further 25 bps hike would accelerate FII equity outflows beyond the current ₹49,935 cr / month pace.

03
Liquidity Conditions

Domestic Flows Hold the Line — But the Fuel Is Thinning

The facts:

  • SIP inflows at ₹32,490 cr / month (95th percentile) — rising 3 consecutive months — with SIP AUM at ₹17.86 lakh cr growing 40% faster than account additions, indicating affluent capital, not retail breadth.
  • DII equity net at ₹51,829 cr / month (96th percentile) is absorbing FII outflows of ₹49,935 cr / month (14th percentile) — the offset is nearly perfect, but it is not a buffer, it is a tightrope.
  • But MF AUM fell to ₹82.64 lakh cr from ₹87.08 lakh cr — a ₹4.44 lakh cr decline — suggesting mark-to-market erosion is quietly reducing the dry powder available for future absorption.
  • NFO collections at ₹998 cr / month (6th percentile) confirm retail is not deploying fresh risk capital into new ideas — existing SIP mandates are the entire story.

The tension: Domestic flows are mechanically offsetting FII exits, but the offset depends on SIP autopilot continuing — any equity market correction that triggers SIP cancellations removes the only buyer of last resort.

Bottom line: If Nifty 50 corrects below 23,500, monitor SIP cancellation rates — a 10% rise in cancellations would reduce monthly DII absorption capacity by an estimated ₹3,000–4,000 cr / month.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

Bank Nifty at All-Time Highs — REITs Pricing a Different Office Market

The facts:

  • NRI residential buying is FALLING — NRI share at 18% of purchases (prior 15%, but down 2 pp vs earlier print); NRI share within premium/luxury at 32% — the segment most exposed to INR depreciation and global carry unwind.
  • Hyderabad leads city momentum: sales +15% YoY, launches +120% YoY, only 28 months unsold inventory — GCC-driven office absorption at 4.1 mn sq ft / quarter (+92% YoY) is the demand anchor.
  • Bank Nifty at 61,519 (100th percentile, all-time high) prices banking strength; but office REIT unit prices tell the opposite story — Embassy REIT at ₹370/unit, Mindspace at ₹345/unit, Brookfield at ₹263/unit, all down 10–28% from prior prints.
  • But overall office absorption collapsed to 4.02 mn sq ft / quarter from 14.1 mn sq ft — a 71% single-quarter drop — with GCC occupiers at 42% of the mix; if GCC expansion pauses, the vacancy floor disappears.

The tension: Bank Nifty prices a credit upcycle that deposit growth cannot sustain; REIT prices discount an office market that GCC demand is still supporting in Hyderabad and Bengaluru — the market is wrong in at least one of these two places.

Bottom line: Avoid office REITs until absorption data confirms the Q3 collapse was seasonal, not structural; watch Hyderabad GCC leasing as the leading indicator — below 3 mn sq ft / quarter signals the thesis breaks.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Four Fuses, One Detonator — Sequencing Matters

The facts:

  • Fuel inflation at 22.93% YoY (95th percentile) with Brent at $95.25/bbl — every $10 adds ~$15 bn to India's annual import bill, widening CAD and pressuring INR beyond its current ₹94.34 (89th percentile).
  • FII equity outflows at ₹49,935 cr / month (14th percentile) with BOJ at a 10-year high 1.25% p.a. — carry unwind has structural momentum; DII absorption is not infinite.
  • CD ratio at 81% (71st percentile) with corporate bond issuance at a 4th-percentile ₹18,700 cr / month — NBFC funding stress is not hypothetical; it is visible in the primary market.
  • But US 10Y at 4.98% yield (90th percentile) rising alongside US GDP at 3.4% SAAR — if the Fed re-accelerates, the RBI's pause becomes untenable regardless of domestic core CPI.

The tension: Each risk is individually manageable; the danger is sequencing — a Brent spike forces CAD widening, INR depreciation accelerates FII exits, DII absorption falters, and the RBI is forced to choose between defending the rupee and supporting growth.

Bottom line: Watch Brent crude this week — a sustained print above $100/bbl triggers the CAD-INR-FII cascade and forces the RBI's hand before the December policy meeting.

NRI Bid Fading — Top-End Supply Building

The facts:

  • Launch mix (supply): 38% of new launches priced above ₹1.5 cr; affordable just 14% — sales split by band not published this print
  • City momentum: Hyderabad leads at 15% sales YoY (prices 6% YoY); Chennai trails at -10%
  • NRI buying is FALLING: 18% of residential purchases (-2 pp vs earlier fetched print); 32% within premium/luxury; concentrated in Mumbai MMR, Bengaluru, Hyderabad
  • Office: Hyderabad leads leasing at 4.1 mn sq ft / quarter (92% YoY); GCCs take 42% of leasing

The tension: NRI share is falling while the top-end bands are adequately supplied — the price support the market has leaned on is thinning.

Bottom line: Watch luxury launch shares: if developers keep launching into the top end as NRI demand fades, unsold inventory in the ₹1.5 cr+ bands is the next print to turn.
