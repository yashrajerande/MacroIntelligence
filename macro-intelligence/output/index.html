01
India Macro Regime

7.8% GDP, But the Consumption Engine Is Losing Torque

The facts:

  • GDP at 7.8% YoY and PMI Composite at 55.9 signal expansion — but the RBI's own forecast of 6.7% YoY implies a 110bps statistical gap between headline and ground-level output.
  • IIP Capital Goods at 16.9% YoY (second consecutive month of acceleration) and capacity utilisation at 77.4% of capacity confirm the investment cycle is real.
  • But e-commerce GMV growth at 12.4% YoY (15th percentile, 10-year low) and passenger vehicle sales decelerating to 7.9% YoY for two consecutive months signal urban discretionary demand is fading.
  • Two-wheeler sales at 33.1% YoY — the informal economy's consumption barometer — remain the lone consumption bright spot, masking weakness in the formal demand chain.

The tension: The investment cycle and the consumption cycle are diverging: capex indicators are at multi-year highs while the discretionary demand proxies that fund corporate earnings growth are softening. A capex upcycle without consumption follow-through stalls at the revenue line.

Bottom line: Watch passenger vehicle sales: a third consecutive month below 10% YoY would confirm consumption deceleration is structural, not seasonal, and would pressure consumer discretionary earnings estimates.

02
Global Macro Regime

DM Re-acceleration Narrative Meets a 92nd-Percentile Bond Market

The facts:

  • US PMI Composite at 58.4 (91st percentile, rising two in a row) and Global PMI Composite at 54.3 (84th percentile) signal DM re-acceleration.
  • But US 10Y Treasury at 5.1% yield (92nd percentile) is doing the Fed's tightening for it — real economy stress is embedded in the curve, not the survey.
  • BOJ at 1.25% p.a. (100th percentile, rising two in a row) is the underappreciated tail: every 25bps of BOJ normalisation unwinds yen carry trades that have funded EM equity inflows since 2021.
  • Eurozone CPI at 3.8% YoY (rising two in a row) with ECB deposit rate at 2.5% p.a. (rising two in a row) means European tightening is not done — a second front of global liquidity withdrawal.

The tension: Survey-based growth indicators are pricing re-acceleration; bond markets and central bank rate paths are pricing sustained tightening. One of them is wrong, and the bond market has a better track record of being right first.

Bottom line: BOJ's next meeting is the single most important global liquidity event for India — a 25bps hike from 1.25% p.a. would accelerate yen carry unwind and amplify FII outflows already running at ₹49,935 cr / month.

03
Liquidity Conditions

SIP Autopilot Holds the Market — But the Fuel Tank Is Narrowing

The facts:

  • SIP inflows at ₹32,490 cr / month (93rd percentile, rising three consecutive months) are absorbing FII equity outflows of ₹49,935 cr / month (17th percentile) — but DII net at ₹51,829 cr / month is only just covering the gap.
  • SIP accounts fell to 10.06 mn from 10.45 mn, while SIP AUM rose to ₹17.86 lakh cr — fewer accounts, larger tickets, meaning concentration risk in the retail base is rising.
  • NFO collections at ₹998 cr / month (2nd percentile, 10-year low) signal that institutional product demand has dried up — the smart-money distribution channel is closed.
  • But INR at 95.24 ₹/USD (89th percentile) is weakening despite record DII support — the currency is telling a different story than the equity market.

The tension: Domestic liquidity is mechanically supporting equities through SIP inflows, but the structural signals — falling SIP account counts, collapsed NFO demand, and a weakening rupee despite strong DII flows — suggest the support is narrowing, not broadening.

Bottom line: If SIP inflow growth (currently 10.7% YoY, down from 15% a quarter ago) drops below 8% YoY, the DII buffer against FII outflows compresses materially — watch the November SIP print.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

NRI Demand Holds Premium; Office REITs Price the Absorption Collapse

The facts:

  • NRI residential buying is RISING — NRI share of purchases at 20% (up from 10%, +10 pp), with 25% share within premium/luxury; remittances at $73 bn (12.8% YoY) provide the funding runway.
  • Hyderabad leads city momentum with sales +15% YoY and launches +120% YoY, but 28 months of unsold inventory is a supply overhang that NRI demand alone cannot clear.
  • Office absorption at 4.0 mn sq ft / quarter (0th percentile — lowest on record) has not stopped Bengaluru rents rising to ₹109/sqft/mo and Mumbai to ₹156/sqft/mo — GCC occupiers (41% of leasing) are taking less space but paying more for it.
  • But Embassy REIT at ₹370/unit, Mindspace at ₹345/unit, and Brookfield at ₹263/unit are all down sharply from prior levels — the listed market has already priced the absorption collapse that the rental data has not yet confirmed.

The tension: Physical office rents are rising while listed REIT units are falling — either the rental market is lagging the demand destruction, or REIT unit prices are overshooting the absorption signal. The GCC-driven occupier mix (41%) is the swing factor.

Bottom line: Avoid office REITs until absorption recovers above 8 mn sq ft / quarter; overweight residential developers with NRI-facing premium inventory in Hyderabad and Bengaluru.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Four Fuses, One Detonator — Fuel Inflation Is Lighting Them All

The facts:

  • Fuel inflation at 22.93% YoY (95th percentile, rising) — every sustained quarter at this level adds ~40bps to headline CPI and forces the RBI to abandon its pause narrative.
  • FII equity outflows at ₹49,935 cr / month (17th percentile) with INR at 95.24 ₹/USD (89th percentile) — carry compression from US 10Y at 5.1% yield makes further outflows the path of least resistance.
  • CD ratio at 81% (73rd percentile) with credit growing at 19.4% YoY vs deposits at 17.8% YoY — a macro-prudential tightening circular on NBFC or unsecured lending would reprice the credit cycle abruptly.
  • BOJ at 1.25% p.a. (100th percentile) — a further hike accelerates yen carry unwind, amplifying FII outflows and INR depreciation simultaneously.

The tension: Each risk is individually manageable; the danger is sequencing — fuel inflation forces RBI hawkishness, which compresses growth, which widens the fiscal deficit, which pressures INR, which amplifies FII outflows, which the DII buffer may no longer fully absorb.

Bottom line: Watch the October CPI print (due ~12 Nov): a second consecutive month above 5.5% YoY would close the door on any rate cut and open the debate on a hike — the threshold that reprices every asset class simultaneously.

NRI Bid Rising — Top End Supplied

The facts:

  • Affordable (< ₹40 lakh) took 16% of sales vs 14% of launches — supply and demand matched
  • City momentum: Hyderabad leads at 15% sales YoY; Chennai trails at -10%
  • NRI buying is RISING: 20% of residential purchases (+10 pp vs prior period in source); 25% within premium/luxury; concentrated in Mumbai Metropolitan Region, Bengaluru, Hyderabad
  • Office: Bengaluru leads leasing at 5.2 mn sq ft / quarter (11% YoY); GCCs take 41% of leasing

The tension: Supply is following demand into the same bands with a lag; the balance shifts one quarter after the sales mix does.

Bottom line: Track the launch share of the undersupplied bands next quarter — supply catching up is what turns a price story into an inventory story.
