01
India Macro Regime

7.8% GDP with a PMI Composite at 56.5 — down from 58.9 — describes an economy that is expanding but losing velocity at the margin. Core sector at 2.3% YoY, down from 4.7%, is the proxy that matters: it covers the physical sinews of the economy — cement, steel, electricity — and its deceleration contradicts both the IIP capital goods rebound and the budget's capex narrative. Consumption signals are bifurcating sharply: CV sales at 28.89% YoY and GST at ₹2,00,640 cr point to a physical economy in motion, while airline passengers at a 10-year low percentile and e-commerce GMV growth halving to 12.4% signal that the discretionary digital consumer has already begun to retrench. India's dual economy is running two separate cycles simultaneously — the formal, physical economy is accelerating; the aspirational, digital consumer is not.

02
Global Macro Regime

S&P 500 at 7,785 — a 100th-percentile all-time high — while US GDP prints 0.7% SAAR, down from 4.4%, is the central contradiction in global macro today. US PMI Composite at 50.3 is barely in expansion; US core PCE at 3.1%, up from 3.0%, means the Fed's preferred inflation gauge is re-accelerating even as growth stalls — a combination that forecloses both the rate-cut and the soft-landing narrative simultaneously. Nikkei at a 100th-percentile 68,714, rising five sessions in a row, is pricing a yen-carry and export-earnings story that depends on the BOJ staying at 0.75% — any policy surprise in Tokyo transmits to EM equity outflows within 48 hours. China GDP at 4.5%, down from 5.0%, with CPI at only 1.3%, confirms the deflationary demand gap that keeps commodity prices capped from the demand side — the only upside risk to Brent is geopolitical, not Chinese industrial recovery.

03
Liquidity Conditions

FII equity net flows swung to -₹114 cr from +₹22,615 cr the prior month — a ₹22,729 cr reversal in a single period — while DII absorption at +₹128 cr is insufficient to fill the gap by two orders of magnitude. SIP inflows at ₹29,845 cr (85th percentile) remain the structural backstop, but SIP account growth at 1.55% YoY — a 2nd-percentile crawl — means the inflow pool is deepening from existing investors, not widening from new ones; that is a concentration risk, not a broadening. INR at 95.24 (89th percentile) is stable only because RBI intervention is visible in the data — the intra-month range of 95.24 to 96.15 with FII selling confirms the central bank is absorbing pressure that the market is not pricing. Corporate bond issuance at ₹1,12,000 cr (7th percentile historically) signals credit rationing at the bank level — when corporates tap bond markets at distressed-percentile volumes, the banking system's willingness to lend has already contracted ahead of any regulatory action.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

Nifty50 at 24,366 (78th percentile) has fallen five of the last six sessions while Bank Nifty at 57,491 (95th percentile) sits near all-time highs — a divergence that is arithmetically unstable: if CD ratio tightening triggers macro-prudential action on risk weights, Bank Nifty reprices and drags the index. Real estate is bifurcating by segment: residential sales at 1,01,675 units are down from 1,08,970 while launches at 1,26,265 units are up — unsold inventory has risen to 6,00,000 units, a supply overhang that will pressure pricing in the next two to three quarters even as affordability improves on lower home loan rates of 8.0%. Listed REITs are signalling stress that the physical market has not yet acknowledged — Embassy REIT down 17.6% from 449 to 369, Mindspace down 30.2%, Brookfield down 23.9% — while office absorption at 29.9 mn sqft and vacancy at 13.9% (down from 17.2%) suggest the physical office market is tightening; the REIT discount to NAV is either a liquidity premium or a leading indicator of physical market stress — the answer arrives in the next quarterly absorption data.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Three risks, ranked by probability times impact: first, Brent at $88.52 and rising three of the last five weeks — a sustained move above $90 adds ₹2-3 lakh cr to India's annual import bill, widens CAD by approximately 40bps per $10, and forces RBI to choose between defending INR (selling reserves) or allowing pass-through inflation (abandoning the rate-cut optionality the market is still pricing). Second, the CD ratio at 83.04% — if deposit growth does not accelerate to close the 300bps gap with credit growth, the RBI's next move is a risk-weight circular on unsecured lending, not a rate cut; Bank Nifty at a 95th percentile is not priced for that outcome. Third, the consumption bifurcation — airline passengers at a 7th-percentile low and e-commerce GMV growth at 12.4% (15th percentile) diverging from physical retail and vehicle sales is the kind of leading indicator that preceded the 2019 consumption slowdown by approximately two quarters; if the digital consumer's retrenchment spreads to physical durables, the GST beat reverses and the growth narrative loses its most credible proxy. Watch Friday's weekly FII flow data — if the ₹22,729 cr reversal extends for a second consecutive period, INR stability becomes the week's dominant story.
