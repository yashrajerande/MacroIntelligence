01
India Macro Regime

7.8% GDP growth sits alongside a 2.3% YoY core sector print — the weakest in the dataset — and that gap is the most important number in today's brief. PMI Composite fell to 56.5 from 58.9, with manufacturing down to 53.9 from 56.9; the expansion is real but decelerating at the margin. Two-wheeler sales at 28.68% and PV sales at 26.1% suggest the formal consumption economy is holding, yet airline traffic at a 7th-percentile 35 million and e-commerce GMV growth halving to 12.4% point to a bifurcation — the affluent formal economy is fine; the mass-market discretionary economy is not. India's FY estimate has been revised up to 7.6% from 7.1%, but the RBI's own forecast of 6.9% is the more conservative anchor, and core sector data supports the RBI's caution over the consensus upgrade.

02
Global Macro Regime

US GDP SAAR at 0.7% — down from 4.4% — while S&P 500 hits a 100th-percentile 7,712, Nikkei a 100th-percentile 66,406, and Euro Stoxx 50 a 100th-percentile 6,486: three simultaneous all-time equity highs against the weakest US growth print in six quarters is a divergence that resolves in one of two ways, and equities are betting on the benign one. US 10Y at 4.67% (84th percentile) with US core PCE ticking up to 3.1% from 3.0% means the Fed's real rate is positive and tightening at the margin — not the backdrop that justifies equity multiples at historical extremes. China GDP at 4.5% (down from 5.0%) with CPI at 1.3% (up from 0.2%) suggests a domestic reflation attempt is underway, which explains iron ore's 69% single-period spike to $161.91/tonne — but one month of commodity data is not a China recovery thesis. Global PMI Composite at 51.0 (49th percentile) is the honest summary: the world economy is barely expanding, and equity markets have priced a different world.

03
Liquidity Conditions

FII equity net flows reversed to -₹114 crore from +₹22,615 crore in the prior period — a ₹22,729 crore single-period swing that is the sharpest reversal in the dataset — while DII absorption fell to a 22nd-percentile ₹128 crore, meaning domestic institutions did not step in to absorb the FII exit. SIP inflows at ₹29,845 crore (85th percentile) remain structurally supportive, but SIP account additions have stalled at a 2nd-percentile 10.45 crore — the pipeline of new retail investors is not replenishing at the rate the AUM figures suggest. CD ratio at 83.04% with corporate bond issuance at a 7th-percentile ₹1.12 lakh crore signals that the banking system's funding stress is pushing borrowers into a bond market that is itself thin — a liquidity squeeze that tightens from both ends. India VIX at a 5th-percentile 10.68 prices none of this; complacency at this level has historically preceded volatility episodes within two to three months.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

Nifty at 24,176 (78th percentile) and Bank Nifty at 57,496 (95th percentile) are pricing a credit cycle that the deposit base structurally cannot support — NIM compression from deposit rate competition is the second-order effect the market has not yet priced. REIT valuations tell a different story: Embassy at ₹369.92 (down from ₹437.19), Mindspace at ₹345.06 (down from ₹487.55), and Brookfield at ₹263.49 (down from ₹341.39) — all three down sharply, consistent with office absorption at a 24th-percentile 29.9 million sqft and retail mall vacancy rising to 5.7% from 5.1%. Residential real estate shows a more nuanced picture: home loan rates down to 8% from 9.1% have lifted the affordability index to 0.61 from 0.46, but sales units fell to 101,675 from 108,970 even as launches rose to 126,265 — unsold inventory is building at 600,000 units. Bengaluru office rents at ₹102/sqft (up from ₹93.6) are the one commercial real estate signal that hasn't capitulated, sustained by IT sector demand that the flat Nifty IT reading suggests is not accelerating.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

First, US 10Y at 4.67% with core PCE at 3.1% and rising: if the Fed is forced to hold or hike while US GDP is at 0.7% SAAR, the global risk-off that follows will test India's ₹114 crore FII outflow as a trickle before a flood — DXY at 99.68 and rising is the early warning. Second, the CD ratio at 83.04% with NBFC credit growth decelerating to 13% from 17%: a macro-prudential circular on risk weights — the RBI's preferred tool — would reprice the entire NBFC and small-bank credit stack within a quarter. Third, food CPI at 3.47% up from 2.13% in a single period: if the monsoon tail-risk materialises in September data, the RBI's revised forecast of 4.6% becomes a floor, not a ceiling, and the rate-cut narrative that equity multiples are partly pricing evaporates. Fourth, the airline traffic and e-commerce divergence from GST and vehicle sales: if the mass-market demand cliff is real and not seasonal, Q2 corporate earnings — particularly consumer staples and retail — will disappoint against consensus. Watch Friday's RBI liquidity data and next week's NBFC funding cost disclosures — those two prints will determine whether the credit stress is contained or contagious.
