Executive Summary
▾
01
India Macro Regime

7.8% GDP with a 2.3% core sector — an 18-month low — is the central tension in India's macro today. PMI Composite at 56.5 has decelerated from 58.9, with Manufacturing sliding to 53.9 from 56.9 — the sub-index most correlated with actual goods production. IIP Capital Goods at 12.5% YoY sounds like investment acceleration, but core sector measures what actually moved through the economy in cement, steel, and electricity — and that number is 2.3%, down from 4.7%. ₹2,00,640 crore in GST collections confirms nominal demand is real, but the composition — vehicle sales surging while airline traffic hits a decade low — signals a consumption base that is narrowing, not broadening.

02
Global Macro Regime

US GDP at 0.7% SAAR — down from 4.4% the prior quarter, a 370bps deceleration — is the number the S&P 500 at an all-time 7,747 is choosing to ignore. US core PCE at 3.1% is rising, the Fed funds rate is flat at 3.625%, and US 10Y Treasuries at 4.79% — the 86th percentile, rising four of the last five periods — are pricing a higher-for-longer reality that equity multiples have not yet absorbed. Global PMI Composite at 51 — barely above contraction — with China GDP at 4.5% and Eurozone GDP at 0.9% confirms this is a liquidity-driven equity rally, not a growth-driven one; copper at $6.67/lb is the one commodity that should not be at the 80th percentile in this growth environment, and its strength is either a leading indicator of a recovery nobody else sees, or a positioning overshoot waiting to correct.

03
Liquidity Conditions

FII equity net flows at -₹114 crore — a reversal from +₹22,615 crore the prior month — with DII support at a thin ₹128 crore (22nd percentile) means domestic institutional absorption is not filling the foreign exit. SIP inflows at ₹29,845 crore remain at the 85th percentile, but SIP accounts at 10.45 crore are at the 2nd percentile for growth — the inflow base is concentrating, not expanding, with average ticket size rising as mass-market participation stalls. INR at 94.34 — the 87th percentile — has stabilised after oscillating between 94.34 and 96.15 over six periods, but US 10Y at 4.79% keeps carry-trade unwind pressure alive; a DXY recovery from its current 99.0 would reopen that pressure immediately. Corporate bond issuance at ₹1,12,000 crore — 7th percentile historically — signals that NBFCs are not choosing wholesale markets; they are being pushed there by a deposit system that cannot keep pace with credit demand at 13.8% YoY.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

Nifty50 at 23,873 — falling five consecutive sessions from 24,208 — while Bank Nifty at 57,381 sits at the 95th percentile creates a specific risk: Bank Nifty's elevation is priced on credit growth of 13.8%, but that credit growth is running into a CD ratio of 83.04% — and the last time the regulator saw this combination, risk weights on unsecured lending followed within two quarters. Real estate shows a clean bifurcation: office vacancy at 13.9% (down from 17.2%) and Bengaluru rents at ₹102/sqft (up from ₹93.6) confirm commercial demand is genuine, while residential unsold inventory at 6,00,000 units — up from prior — and retail mall vacancy rising to 5.7% from 5.1% signal that the residential and retail segments are absorbing less than launches are adding. REITs — Embassy at ₹369.92, Mindspace at ₹345.06, Brookfield at ₹263.49 — are all trading well below prior levels despite improving office fundamentals, a dislocation that either prices in rate-cut delays or anticipates NIM compression in the financial sector that funds them.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Brent at $95.93 — up four of five sessions, now at the 68th percentile — is the highest-probability near-term risk: every $10 sustained above $90 adds approximately ₹8,000 crore per month to India's import bill, widens CAD by 40bps, and feeds back into CPI within two quarters — at which point the RBI's 4.6% inflation forecast, already revised up from 4.2%, becomes a floor rather than a ceiling. Second, the CD ratio at 83.04% climbing from 82.5% for three consecutive months is approaching the threshold where macro-prudential intervention becomes the path of least resistance for the RBI — a risk-weight circular on unsecured or NBFC lending would reprice Bank Nifty at its 95th-percentile valuation with no warning. Third, the US growth deceleration to 0.7% SAAR from 4.4% — if it deepens — would compress global PMI from its already-thin 51, triggering a risk-off that India's -₹114 crore FII base cannot absorb without INR pressure. Watch Friday's US non-farm payrolls: a sub-100K print would force the market to price US recession risk into a yield curve that is currently at the 86th percentile — and that repricing would not be orderly.

Rotate out of discretionary retail, e-commerce, and aviation stocks immediately. Overweight FMCG, utilities, and healthcare. The airline collapse is particularly concerning because it's a high-frequency, real-time proxy for business and leisure travel — a leading indicator of corporate capex and consumer confidence. If this persists for 2 more months, expect GDP growth to decelerate to 6.5-7% by Q4 FY27. Falsification: if airline traffic rebounds to >37mn by next month and e-commerce GMV growth re-accelerates to >20%, this signal is invalidated.
