Executive Summary
▾
01
India Macro Regime

7.8% GDP growth with a PMI Composite at 56.5 — both decelerating from prior readings of 8.4% and 58.9 respectively — describes an economy that is still expanding but losing altitude. Core sector at 2.3% YoY, the lowest in 18 months, is the more honest read of physical output: cement, steel, and electricity generation do not lie the way deflators can. GST at ₹2,00,640 cr confirms consumption is holding, but the split between PV sales at +26.1% and e-commerce GMV growth at 12.4% (halved from 25%) maps a two-speed economy — affluent and accelerating at the top, softening in the middle.

02
Global Macro Regime

US GDP SAAR at 1.5% — down from 2.1% — while S&P 500 sits at a 100th-percentile 7,718 is the central contradiction in global markets today. US 10Y at 4.77% (86th percentile) has risen four of the last five periods — the bond market is pricing higher-for-longer while equities price a soft landing — and both cannot be correct simultaneously. Brent at $96.28 (up four of five sessions from $88.10) adds a commodity inflation overlay that the Fed, with core PCE still at 3.34%, cannot ignore; US VIX at a 8th-percentile 14.53 suggests markets have not begun to price the resolution of this contradiction.

03
Liquidity Conditions

FII equity net flows turned negative at -₹114 cr — a sharp reversal from +₹22,615 cr prior — while DII absorption at ₹128 cr is at a 22nd-percentile low, meaning domestic institutions are not stepping in with conviction. SIP inflows at ₹29,845 cr remain at the 85th percentile, but SIP account growth at 1.55% MoM (a 2nd-percentile pace) confirms that incremental flows are coming from larger tickets, not new participants — a structurally narrower base than the headline suggests. Corporate bond issuance at ₹1,12,000 cr (7th percentile) signals that firms are accessing capital markets aggressively, likely anticipating that bank credit — constrained by a 83.04% CD ratio — will become more expensive or less available.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

Nifty 50 at 23,897 has fallen five consecutive sessions from 24,208, while Bank Nifty at 57,369 (95th percentile) holds near its highs — a divergence that is difficult to sustain if CD ratio pressure translates into NIM compression and risk-weight tightening. Retail mall vacancy at a 15th-percentile 6.7% — tightest in three years — and office absorption at 26.9 mn sqft (driven by GCCs, not traditional IT) tell a real estate story that is bifurcated by asset class: commercial and experiential retail are tight, while residential launches at a 0th-percentile 106,000 units and an affordability index at a 19th-percentile 3.5 signal that the housing market is pricing out the mass buyer. REIT prices — Embassy down to ₹369 from ₹438, Mindspace to ₹345 from ₹501 — suggest the office absorption story is not yet translating into investor returns.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Three risks, ranked by probability × impact: First, Brent sustaining above $95 — already at $96.28 for four consecutive sessions — pushes fuel CPI from -3.78% to positive within 60 days, takes headline CPI above 4%, and eliminates any residual case for a rate cut while widening CAD by approximately 40bps per $10 move. Second, CD ratio at 83.04% triggering a macro-prudential response — risk-weight increases on unsecured or NBFC lending — which would compress NBFC credit growth (already down from 17% to 13%) and reprice the mid-cap financials that dominate active fund portfolios. Third, US 10Y at 4.77% with VIX at 14.53 (8th percentile) is the classic setup for a volatility event — if the Fed signals higher-for-longer more forcefully, carry unwinds hit INR and FII flows simultaneously, testing RBI's reserve management. Watch Thursday's US CPI print: a surprise above 3.5% is the single data point most likely to detonate all three risks in sequence.
