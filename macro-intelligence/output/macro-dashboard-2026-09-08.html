Executive Summary

01
India Macro Regime

7.8% GDP growth with a 6.6% RBI forecast is not a rounding error — it is a 120bps institutional disagreement about what is actually happening in the economy. PMI composite at 54.6 (83rd percentile) flatters on the surface; manufacturing at 52.8 is softening while services at 54.5 carries the load — a services-led expansion that historically proves more fragile when global demand slows. Capacity utilisation at 77.4% is rising and IIP capital goods at 16% YoY is genuine, but core sector deceleration to 5.4% from 6.0% suggests the goods economy is not uniformly confirming the headline. GST at ₹2,00,640 cr and two-wheeler sales at 19.7% are the two proxies that keep the consumption story credible — watch both for any sequential softening.

02
Global Macro Regime

US GDP at 1.5% SAAR — down from 2.1% — is slowing while US PMI composite hits 56 (84th percentile), a divergence that usually resolves in the PMI's direction with a lag, or exposes the PMI as a sentiment survey rather than an activity measure. BOJ at 1.0% (86th percentile, up from 0.75%) is the structural shift the market keeps underpricing — every 25bps from Tokyo tightens global yen carry and pressures EM assets that benefited from it. US 10Y at 4.77% has risen four of the last five periods; the Fed is on hold at 3.63% while the long end prices its own verdict on fiscal sustainability. China at 4.7% GDP and 0.5% CPI is the deflationary anchor that keeps global goods prices suppressed — until it isn't.

03
Liquidity Conditions

SIP inflows at ₹31,961 cr (91st percentile) are structurally robust, but equity MF net flows fell to ₹24,697 cr from ₹28,973 cr — a ₹4,276 cr sequential decline that suggests lump-sum investors are stepping back even as SIP automation holds. FII equity net at -₹3,112 cr against DII net of +₹8,930 cr means domestic institutions are absorbing foreign selling — a pattern that can persist until DII dry powder thins. Corporate bond issuance at ₹1,12,000 cr (7th percentile) signals that credit-constrained borrowers are paying up for capital market access as the CD ratio at 83.04% leaves banks with limited deposit headroom. INR at 94.34 (87th percentile) has stabilised after oscillating between 94.34 and 96.15 over five periods — the calm is real but the range is not reassuring.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

Nifty 50 at 23,779 (76th percentile) has risen only once in the last five sessions — a market that is not falling but is not finding conviction either, with Bank Nifty at 57,088 (94th percentile) carrying valuation risk if NIM compression follows from deposit rate competition. Office rents in Mumbai at ₹335/sqft (100th percentile) and Bengaluru at ₹163/sqft (95th percentile) are at decade extremes — yet absorption at 13.7 mn sqft sits at the 6th percentile, and REIT prices tell the real story: Embassy down to ₹369.92 from ₹438.69, Mindspace to ₹345.06 from ₹501.11. Residential real estate launches at a 0th-percentile 106,000 units and sales at 90,715 units (0th percentile) with unsold inventory rising to 6,16,000 units suggest the residential cycle is turning — home loan disbursements at ₹2,59,000 cr are holding up only because rates have fallen to 8.12%. Gold in INR at ₹13,518/gram (100th percentile) is the quiet hedge that institutional India is running alongside its equity exposure.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Three risks, ranked by probability times impact. First: Brent at $97.21 — up from $88.82 six months ago — sustaining above $95 adds approximately ₹24,000 cr to India's monthly import bill, widens CAD by 40bps per $10, and forces the RBI to choose between defending INR and continuing its rate-cut cycle; fuel inflation at 27.41% (100th percentile) has not yet fed fully into core. Second: CD ratio at 83.04% with NBFC credit growth decelerating to 13% from 17% — if deposit growth stays at 10.8% while credit demand holds at 13.8%, the regulator's next move is macro-prudential tightening via risk weights, not rate policy. Third: BOJ at 1.0% with US 10Y sticky at 4.77% — yen carry unwind pressure on EM flows is not priced into India VIX at 11.16 (6th percentile). Watch Friday's US CPI print — at 3.30% headline with core PCE flat at 3.34%, any upside surprise resets the Fed pause narrative and the entire EM carry trade simultaneously.
