Executive Summary
▾
01
India Macro Regime

7.8% GDP growth with a 6.7% RBI forecast creates a 110bps credibility gap that three proxies fail to close: IIP at 6.7% (down from 8.8%), core sector at 5.4% (down from 6.0%), and manufacturing PMI at 52.8 (retreating from 53.5) — all pointing to deceleration, not acceleration. Capacity utilisation at 77.4% and IIP capital goods at 16.1% are the genuine bright spots, confirming an investment cycle that is real and measurable. Fuel inflation at a 95th-percentile 22.93% — up from 20.05% — is the variable that breaks the benign narrative: every additional month of Brent above $105 adds ₹2-3 trillion annualised to India's import bill and feeds back into CPI with a 2-quarter lag. Two-wheeler sales at 19.7% YoY suggest rural demand is recovering, but credit card growth at a 26th-percentile 2.3% signals urban discretionary is already showing fatigue.

02
Global Macro Regime

US 10Y at 4.96% (90th percentile, up four of the last five months) is the single most consequential external variable today — not because of what it does to US growth, but because of what it does to EM carry trades funded in dollars. BOJ at 1.0% (up from 0.75%) is compressing the yen carry simultaneously — the last time both the US long end and BOJ rates rose together, EM outflows were measured in hundreds of billions over two quarters. China's PMI composite at 49.5 (below 50, down from 52.1 two months ago) and GDP at 4.3% (down from 5.0%) remove the one global demand engine that could have offset US tightening — Nikkei at a 100th-percentile 63,493 falling alongside Euro Stoxx at a 100th-percentile 6,260 suggests developed market equity is beginning to price what bond markets have been saying for four months. US VIX at 17.1 (rising three of the last five sessions) is still low in absolute terms but the direction matters more than the level at this stage of the cycle.

03
Liquidity Conditions

DII equity net at an all-time-high ₹58,502 crore (100th percentile) — absorbing FII inflows of ₹29,631 crore that reversed sharply from -₹3,112 crore the prior month — means domestic institutional money is providing a liquidity floor that masks the underlying fragility. SIP inflows at ₹32,297 crore (rising for two consecutive months) with AUM at ₹18.62 trillion (93rd percentile) confirm the structural domestication of Indian equity flows, but SIP YoY growth decelerating to 14% (from 16%, 36th percentile) is the leading indicator that this floor has a ceiling. Bank credit at 17.1% against deposit growth of 10.8% — a 630bps gap — means the banking system is funding credit expansion through non-deposit sources, and NBFC credit growth accelerating to 14.9% (from 10.6%) signals wholesale funding markets are being stretched to compensate.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

Nifty at 23,398 (75th percentile, down five of the last six sessions) with India VIX at a 7th-percentile 12.3 is a positioning mismatch — global equity stress (Nikkei -1,500 points, Euro Stoxx at multi-month lows) is not yet reflected in domestic hedging costs. Bank Nifty at 56,607 (93rd percentile) is the most exposed index: a CD ratio at 81.4% with credit growing at 17.1% is the setup for a macro-prudential circular on risk weights — the last time the RBI moved on risk weights (November 2023 on unsecured lending), Bank Nifty fell 8% in six weeks. REIT prices tell the real estate story more honestly than rent indices: Embassy at ₹369.92 (down from ₹439.07), Mindspace at ₹345.06 (down from ₹491.66), and Brookfield at ₹263.49 (down from ₹335.41) — all three down 15-30% from prior levels — while office absorption at a 0th-percentile 4.9 mn sqft confirms the GCC demand that was pricing those rents has not materialised at scale.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Ranked by probability × impact: first, Brent at $106.42 (rising five consecutive months) sustaining above $105 — every additional month adds ₹2-3 trillion to India's annualised import bill, widens CAD by 40bps, and puts the INR at 95.24 (89th percentile) under pressure that RBI intervention alone cannot contain indefinitely. Second, US 10Y at 4.96% triggering an EM carry unwind — FII equity inflows reversed sharply last month and the BOJ hike to 1.0% adds a second carry-trade compression vector that the consensus has not fully priced. Third, the CD ratio at 81.4% with NBFC credit at 14.9% YoY inviting a macro-prudential response — risk weight increases on NBFC lending or unsecured credit would reprice the entire credit cycle within two quarters. Fourth, India VIX at a 7th-percentile 12.3 while global VIX rises — the gap between domestic complacency and global stress historically closes in one direction. Watch US 10Y at the 5.0% threshold this week: a clean break above it is the single data point most likely to cascade across all four risks simultaneously.
