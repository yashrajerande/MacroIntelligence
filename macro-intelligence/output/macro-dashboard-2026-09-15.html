Executive Summary
▾
01
India Macro Regime

7.8% GDP growth with a 7.7% full-year estimate sounds like a clean acceleration — until IIP at 6.7% (down from 8.8%) and core sector at 5.4% (down from 6.0%) reveal that goods-economy momentum is actually fading beneath the headline. PMI composite at 54.6 is at the 83rd percentile, but manufacturing slipped to 52.8 — the services sector is carrying a number that the formal goods economy isn't confirming. Capacity utilisation at 77.4% and IIP capital goods at 16.1% are the genuine bright spots: at the 74th percentile utilisation threshold, private capex announcements historically follow within two to three quarters. The risk is that fuel inflation at 22.93% — 95th percentile — compresses corporate margins before that capex cycle fully ignites.

02
Global Macro Regime

US 10Y at 4.96% — 90th percentile, up four of the last five months — is the single most important external variable for India today, repricing EM carry trades and widening the interest rate differential that determines FII behaviour. US GDP SAAR has slowed to 1.5% from 2.1%, yet the Fed holds at 3.63% with core PCE at 3.34% — policy is restrictive relative to growth, not just inflation. The BOJ at 1.0% (86th percentile, up from 0.75%) is unwinding the last pillar of global liquidity suppression — yen carry unwind risk is not priced in India's 8th-percentile VIX at 13.4. China's composite PMI at 49.5 with GDP at 4.3% (down from 5.0%) removes the commodity demand tailwind that India's export sector was quietly relying on.

03
Liquidity Conditions

DII equity net at ₹58,502 crore — an all-time high, 100th percentile — is absorbing every wave of FII hesitation, with SIP inflows at ₹32,297 crore rising for two consecutive months and SIP AUM at ₹18.62 trillion (93rd percentile). Domestic liquidity is structurally deep, but the banking system's funding architecture is under stress: credit at 17.1% against deposits at 10.8% — a 630bps gap — means banks are competing for the same retail savings that are flowing into mutual funds, creating a structural deposit-mobilisation problem that SIP growth actively worsens. NBFC credit growth jumping to 14.9% from 10.6% signals wholesale funding channels are being stretched; corporate bond issuance at ₹18,700 crore sits at the 0th percentile, meaning the capital market is not providing the relief valve it should. The CD ratio at 81.4% — 75th percentile — is the number the RBI watches before reaching for macro-prudential tools.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

Nifty at 23,119 (74th percentile) and Bank Nifty at 55,795 (92nd percentile) are diverging in a way that should concern equity allocators: Bank Nifty's 92nd-percentile valuation sits atop a funding structure — CD ratio at 81.4%, deposit growth at 10.8% — that historically precedes NIM compression and risk-weight tightening. India VIX at 13.4 (8th percentile) prices near-zero tail risk even as US VIX has climbed to 17.6 (12th percentile) over three of the last five sessions — the complacency gap between domestic and global volatility pricing is 9 percentile points and widening. On real estate, the GCC-led office absorption story at 29.9 mn sqft is structurally sound — non-IT corporates in BFSI and manufacturing are the primary demand source — but REIT prices tell a different story: Embassy down 16%, Mindspace down 30%, Brookfield down 21% from prior levels, suggesting the listed market is discounting a rate-for-longer scenario that the physical leasing market hasn't yet absorbed. Credit card growth at 2.3% (down from 5.6%) is the early-warning indicator for consumer discretionary earnings — watch it.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Ranked by probability × impact: first, INR at 96.15 (91st percentile) weakening despite record DII inflows confirms that Brent at $103/bbl is overwhelming the capital account — every $10 sustained on crude adds approximately $15bn to the annual import bill, widens CAD by ~40bps, and feeds back into CPI with a two-quarter lag that the RBI's 5.1% forecast may still be underestimating. Second, US 10Y at 4.96% — if it breaches 5.0%, the historical precedent from Q3 2023 is a sharp EM equity outflow episode; India's FII equity position at ₹29,631 crore net inflow could reverse faster than the DII absorption capacity can offset. Third, the CD ratio at 81.4% with NBFC credit accelerating to 14.9% creates the conditions for a macro-prudential response — risk-weight increases on unsecured lending or NBFC funding norms — that would hit consumption credit and Bank Nifty earnings simultaneously. Fourth, BOJ at 1.0% and rising is the underpriced global risk: yen carry unwind episodes have historically correlated with sharp INR depreciation and India VIX spikes within 4-6 weeks. Watch the US 10Y print this week — a 5.0% close is the single data point that changes the risk calculus for every other variable on this list.
