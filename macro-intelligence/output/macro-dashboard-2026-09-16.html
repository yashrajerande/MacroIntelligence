Executive Summary

01
India Macro Regime

7.8% GDP growth with a 6.7% RBI forecast sitting 110bps below it is not a confidence interval — it is a warning about statistical construction. PMI composite at 54.6 (83rd percentile) flatters: manufacturing slipped to 52.8 while services held at 54.5, meaning the composite is a weighted average of a decelerating goods economy and a still-buoyant services sector that fuel at 22.93% inflation will begin to tax within two quarters. IIP at 6.7% YoY — down from 8.8% — confirms momentum is already fading in the activity data. Capacity utilisation at 77.4% is the one unambiguously positive signal: private capex commitments follow this number with a 2-3 quarter lag, and the threshold has been crossed.

02
Global Macro Regime

US 10Y at 4.97% — rising four of the last five months to a 90th-percentile yield — is the gravitational constant around which every other asset is now orbiting. US GDP at 1.5% SAAR (down from 2.1%) with core CPI falling to 2.45% creates the paradox: the economy is slowing, yet the Fed cannot cut because US headline CPI at 3.35% is rising and the BOJ's rate hike to 1.0% is unwinding the yen carry trade that funded global risk appetite. Euro Stoxx 50 at a 100th-percentile 6,237 — sliding five consecutive sessions — while China GDP decelerates to 4.3% (from 5.0%) and China PMI composite at 49.5 signals contraction: the synchronised global recovery priced into equities is not showing up in the activity data.

03
Liquidity Conditions

DII equity net at ₹58,502 crore — a 100th-percentile print, rising two consecutive months — is the single largest domestic absorption event on record, and it is doing the work that FII equity net at ₹29,631 crore (volatile, up from -₹3,112 crore prior month) cannot be relied upon to sustain. SIP AUM at ₹18.62 lakh crore (93rd percentile) growing 40% faster than SIP account additions signals rising average ticket size — affluent savers are increasing allocation, not new retail participants entering. The structural risk: bank credit at 17.1% against deposit growth of 10.8% means the banking system is funding credit expansion through wholesale markets and NBFC channels — a 630bps divergence that tightens the moment global risk appetite turns.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

Bank Nifty at 55,795 — down from 57,381 five sessions ago, at a 92nd-percentile level — is the index most exposed to the CD ratio fault line: NIM compression from deposit rate competition, macro-prudential risk weight tightening on unsecured lending, and NBFC funding stress are three separate vectors pointing at the same earnings outcome. Nifty 50 at 23,119 (74th percentile, down five of six sessions) with India VIX rising to 13.43 from a 5th-percentile 10.68 three weeks ago signals that the complacency trade is unwinding — slowly, but the direction is set. Office REITs — Embassy at ₹369.92, Mindspace at ₹345.06, Brookfield at ₹263.49 — are all down 15-30% from prior levels despite office absorption rising to 26.9 mn sqft; the REIT derating is a rate story, not a demand story, and it will not reverse until US 10Y peaks.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Three risks, ranked by probability × impact. First: fuel inflation at 22.93% (95th percentile) with Brent at $108.41/bbl — every $10 adds approximately $15bn to India's import bill, widening CAD by 40bps and pushing INR through 97 — forces RBI to hold or tighten precisely when growth is decelerating, a policy trap with no clean exit. Second: CD ratio at 81.4% with NBFC credit at 14.9% YoY — if wholesale funding costs rise 50bps in response to US 10Y at 4.97%, the NBFC segment faces a margin-and-liquidity squeeze simultaneously, the same sequence that preceded the IL&FS stress of 2018. Third: China GDP at 4.3% with PMI composite at 49.5 — a contraction signal — threatens copper at $6.46/lb (76th percentile) and iron ore at $161.91, which would compress India's metals and mining earnings even as domestic capex is accelerating. Watch Friday's US core PCE print at 3.34% — if it rises, the Fed's June cut consensus dissolves and the INR-Brent-CAD chain tightens further.

The inversion worth making: RBI's own GDP forecast of 6.7% sits 110bps below the headline print — the statistical gap between GDP and GVA is doing work that genuine output growth is not.

Inflation Cycle
CPI 4.45%; Fuel 22.93%
CPI at 4.45% is above the 4% midpoint target, but the composition is what should unsettle the RBI. Fuel inflation at 22.93% — a 95th-percentile print, up from 20.05% — is feeding directly into WPI at 9.92%, which means producer margins are being squeezed before the consumer even feels the full pass-through. Core CPI at 3.9% (28th percentile) appears benign, but that divergence between a low core and a spiking fuel component is precisely the setup where second-order effects — transport costs into food prices, logistics repricing — push headline CPI through 5% within two quarters. RBI's own inflation forecast has already moved to 5.1% from 4.6% — the committee is telling you what it sees.

Credit Cycle
Credit 17.1%; CD 81.4%
Bank credit at 17.1% YoY against deposit growth of 10.8% — a 630bps divergence — is the structural fault line. CD ratio at 81.4% has eased one month from 82.5% but remains at the 75th percentile historically; the last time India sustained above 80% while the repo rate was being held was FY14, and that episode ended with emergency macro-prudential tightening. NBFC credit growth accelerating to 14.9% from 10.6% signals that wholesale funding channels are absorbing what retail deposit mobilisation cannot supply — a fragile bridge. Industry credit at 20% YoY and services at 22.9% suggest the demand for credit is genuine, which makes the funding mismatch more dangerous, not less.

Policy Regime

RBI is on hold, but the direction of the next move is being quietly repriced: with US 10Y at 4.97% and INR at a 91st-percentile 96.15, any rate cut would accelerate capital outflows and widen the current account. Housing affordability index at 3.5 (19th percentile) signals that the existing rate level is already compressing real estate demand — a cut would help affordability but arrive too late to prevent the inventory build already underway.

IIP capital goods at 16.1% YoY confirms the commitment is translating into orders.
