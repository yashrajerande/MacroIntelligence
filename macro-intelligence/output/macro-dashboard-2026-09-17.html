Executive Summary
▾
01
India Macro Regime

7.8% GDP growth with a 22.93% fuel inflation print is not a comfortable combination — it is a growth-inflation scissors that leaves the RBI with no clean move. Capacity utilisation at 77.4% and IIP capital goods at 16.1% confirm the investment cycle is real, not statistical; but core sector at 5.4% (down from 6.0%) and manufacturing PMI drifting to 52.8 suggest the cycle's breadth is narrowing even as its headline holds. GST at ₹1,99,853 crore and two-wheeler sales at 19.7% YoY keep consumption from flashing red — but credit card growth at 2.3% (26th percentile) is the trapdoor: recurring discretionary spend is already compressing under fuel's weight. RBI's own inflation forecast revision to 5.1% from 4.6% is the most honest signal in today's data — the central bank is telling you it expects an overshoot, and the market has not priced what that means for the rate path.

02
Global Macro Regime

5.0% on the US 10Y — rising 4 of the last 5 months to a 90th-percentile yield — is the gravitational constant around which every EM asset is now orbiting. US GDP SAAR at 1.5% (down from 2.1%) with US core CPI falling to 2.45% gives the Fed room to cut, but forward guidance remains hawkish — a combination that keeps long rates elevated even as the short end softens. The BOJ's hike to 1.0% (86th percentile) is the structural break: Japanese institutional investors who funded global carry trades at near-zero cost now face a 100bps domestic rate — repatriation flows from this cohort have historically been disorderly, and the Nikkei at a 100th-percentile 63,923 is pricing none of that risk. China's composite PMI at 49.5 and GDP at 4.3% (down from 5.0%) removes the one engine that could have offset tighter DM financial conditions — the global macro setup is simultaneously tighter and slower than three months ago.

03
Liquidity Conditions

₹58,502 crore of DII equity buying — a 100th-percentile monthly print — is the single largest domestic absorption on record, and it is doing the work that FII flows cannot sustain. SIP AUM at ₹18.62 trillion (93rd percentile) is growing 40% faster than SIP account additions, which means average ticket size per account has risen to ₹18.6 lakh from ₹16.4 lakh six months ago — affluent savers are increasing allocation, not just new savers entering. The structural risk: bank deposit growth at 10.8% against credit at 17.1% means the banking system is funding credit expansion by drawing down liquidity buffers, not by attracting new deposits — a dynamic that tightens systemic liquidity even as equity markets appear flush. India VIX at 13.17 (8th percentile) against US VIX at 17.71 (12th percentile) is a complacency gap that historically closes violently when the external shock arrives rather than gradually.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

Bank Nifty at 56,292 (93rd percentile) is pricing a credit upcycle while the CD ratio at 81.4% and NBFC credit growth accelerating to 14.9% are pricing a funding stress — these two cannot both be right for long. Nifty50 at 23,218 has been range-bound for six months (down from 23,914 in the recent sequence) while earnings expectations embed a growth trajectory that a fuel-driven margin squeeze and tighter global rates will test in Q2 results. On real estate, the GCC-versus-traditional-IT divergence in office markets is now showing up in REIT pricing: Embassy REIT at ₹369.92 is down sharply from ₹439.58, Mindspace at ₹345.06 from ₹498.78 — the market is discounting that Bengaluru rent at a 95th-percentile ₹163/sqft is a GCC micro-market story, not a broad office recovery. Home loan rates at 7.5% (32nd percentile, down from 8.75%) are supporting residential demand — disbursements at ₹2,59,000 crore confirm this — but any RBI tightening triggered by fuel inflation would reprice this channel within two quarters.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Ranked by probability × impact: first, Brent above $105 sustained for another month adds approximately $15 billion to India's annualised import bill — CAD widens 40bps, INR depreciates 2-3%, imported inflation feeds CPI with a 2-quarter lag, and RBI faces a forced tightening into a slowing growth cycle. Second, the BOJ hike to 1.0% accelerates yen carry unwind — every 10bps of BOJ tightening historically correlates with 1-2% EM currency depreciation as leveraged positions unwind; INR at a 91st-percentile 96.15 has limited buffer. Third, the CD ratio at 81.4% with NBFC credit at 14.9% growth creates the conditions for a macro-prudential response — risk-weight increases on unsecured and NBFC lending would compress credit growth faster than the market expects, hitting consumption and capex simultaneously. Fourth, India VIX at 13.17 against a global backdrop of US 10Y at 5.0% and BOJ tightening is a mispricing of tail risk — watch the US 10Y at 5.0% for any break above that level, which would be the single most important data point to track before next week's Fed communication.
