01
India Macro Regime

7.8% GDP with composite PMI at 54.6 — an 83rd-percentile reading — looks like a clean growth story until IIP at 6.7% (down from 8.8%) and core sector at 5.4% (down from 6.0%) reveal the deceleration underneath. Capacity utilisation at 77.4% validates the capex narrative — this is real investment, not budget allocation — but manufacturing PMI at 52.8 slipping while services holds at 54.5 means the growth engine is narrowing. Fuel inflation at 22.93% (95th percentile) with WPI at 9.92% is compressing producer margins two to three quarters before consumer prices fully adjust — the earnings risk is deferred, not avoided. Vehicle sales across all three segments running at 16-20% YoY confirm consumption breadth, but credit card growth at 2.3% signals the consumer is choosing asset financing over revolving spend — a durability question dressed as a strength signal.

02
Global Macro Regime

5.01% on the US 10Y — a 91st-percentile yield, rising four of the last five months — is the single most important global price today, and it is moving in the wrong direction for every EM carry trade simultaneously. US PMI composite at 56.0 (84th percentile, up from 54.5) with GDP SAAR at only 1.5% (down from 2.1%) is the classic late-cycle divergence: activity surveys still euphoric, hard output data already softening — the last three US recessions began with exactly this gap. BOJ at 1.0% (25bps hike, 86th percentile) is unwinding the last pillar of global carry, and with Nikkei at a 100th-percentile 64,136, Japanese equities are pricing a soft landing that the bond market — at these yields — is simultaneously pricing as structurally impossible. China composite PMI at 49.5 with GDP at 4.3% (down from 5.0%) removes the one demand buffer that could have offset US softness for Asian exporters.

03
Liquidity Conditions

DII equity net at ₹58,502 crore — a 100th-percentile print, rising two consecutive months — is absorbing every shock the global carry unwind can deliver, with SIP inflows at ₹32,297 crore and MF AUM at ₹87.08 trillion (86th percentile) providing the structural bid. FII equity net at ₹29,631 crore (83rd percentile) has turned positive, but the recent sequence — from -₹3,112 crore to +₹29,631 crore in a single month — is volatile enough to reverse on a single Fed communication. India VIX at 12.29 (7th percentile) against US VIX at 15.44 (9th percentile) is a complacency signal: domestic money is fully deployed, hedges are minimal, and the cushion against a global risk-off event is thinner than the flow data suggests. Bank credit at 17.1% against deposit growth of 10.8% means the system is funding growth through wholesale channels — and wholesale funding is the first to reprice when global rates move.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

Bank Nifty at 56,055 (92nd percentile) with CD ratio at 81.4% and deposit growth trailing credit by 630bps is the most precise valuation tension in Indian equities today — the index is pricing earnings that require deposit mobilisation the system is not delivering. Nifty50 at 23,271 (74th percentile) with India VIX at a 7th-percentile 12.29 means the market is priced for perfection with almost no insurance — a US 10Y at 5.01% sustained through October reprices EM risk premiums by 30-50bps and that flows directly into Nifty multiples. Office absorption at 29.9 mn sqft (24th percentile) with vacancy falling to 13.9% from 17.2% is a genuine improvement, but REIT prices tell the real story: Embassy down to ₹369.92 from ₹439.58, Mindspace at ₹345.06 from ₹498.78 — the physical market is tightening while listed vehicles are being de-rated by rising global yields. Retail mall vacancy at 11.9% confirms consumption is showing up in physical retail, but at these REIT valuations, the yield compression trade is over.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Ranked by probability × impact: first, US 10Y sustaining above 5.0% through the next Fed meeting — at 91st-percentile yields with BOJ tightening simultaneously, EM carry unwinds are non-linear and India's ₹96.15 INR (91st percentile) has limited RBI intervention room without burning reserves. Second, fuel inflation at 22.93% feeding into RBI's forecast revision to 5.1% — if Brent holds at $104, the next MPC meeting closes the door on any accommodation and forces the RBI to choose between INR defence and growth support. Third, the CD ratio at 81.4% with NBFC credit accelerating to 14.9% — the macro-prudential response, when it comes, will arrive via risk-weight circulars on specific segments, and Bank Nifty at a 92nd-percentile valuation has no margin of safety for that event. Fourth, China PMI composite at 49.5 with GDP at 4.3% — a sub-50 composite in the world's largest goods exporter compresses commodity demand and removes the one external demand buffer for Asian supply chains. Watch the RBI's next liquidity management operation: if it shifts from variable rate repo to outright OMO sales, the policy signal will be tighter than the repo rate suggests — that is the single most important data point this week.
