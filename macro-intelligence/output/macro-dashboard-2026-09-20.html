Executive Summary

01
India Macro Regime

7.8% GDP growth with a composite PMI of 54.6 (83rd percentile) looks like an economy in full stride — until IIP at 6.7% (down from 8.8%) and core sector at 5.4% (down from 6.0%) reveal that the acceleration is narrowing, not broadening. Capacity utilisation at 77.4% — up 180bps — is the genuine bright spot, crossing the threshold where private capex conversations move from boardroom to balance sheet. Against this, fuel inflation at 22.93% (95th percentile) and WPI at 9.92% running 550bps above CPI are embedding cost pressure that will surface in corporate margins within two quarters. Two-wheeler sales at +19.7% and CV sales at +16.88% confirm rural and logistics demand is real — but credit card growth slowing to 2.3% from 5.6% signals that mass-market urban discretionary is softer than the vehicle numbers suggest.

02
Global Macro Regime

S&P 500 at 7,650 (100th percentile) with US PMI composite at 56 (84th percentile) is pricing a soft landing with near-certainty — yet US GDP SAAR has decelerated to 1.5% from 2.1%, and US 10Y at 4.94% (89th percentile) is simultaneously pricing a no-cut Fed through year-end. Copper at $6.69/lb rising for four of the last five months (80th percentile) is the commodity market's vote for a global capex upcycle — consistent with iron ore at $161.91 — but China's composite PMI at 49.5 and GDP at 4.3% (down from 5.0%) means the industrial demand signal is coming from the US and India, not from the world's largest commodity consumer. BOJ at 1.0% (86th percentile) tightening while the Fed holds at 3.63% is unwinding the yen carry trade structurally — every basis point of BOJ normalisation repatriates Japanese capital that has been funding EM risk assets for a decade.

03
Liquidity Conditions

DII equity net at ₹58,502 crore (100th percentile, rising two consecutive months) is the dominant domestic liquidity fact — SIP inflows at ₹32,297 crore (92nd percentile) with SIP AUM crossing ₹18.62 trillion (93rd percentile) means the systematic bid is structurally larger than any single month of FII volatility. FII equity net at ₹29,631 crore is positive but the three-month sequence — ₹-114 crore → ₹-3,112 crore → ₹+29,631 crore — is a reversal, not a trend, and US 10Y at 4.94% with no Fed cut in sight limits how durable that reversal can be. Bank CD ratio at 81.4% with deposit growth at 10.8% against credit at 17.1% means the banking system's internal liquidity is being consumed faster than it is being replenished — the equity market's SIP-driven buoyancy coexists with a credit system running on an increasingly thin deposit base. India VIX at 11.39 (6th percentile) signals complacency that is historically a precursor to repricing, not a confirmation of stability.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

Bank Nifty at 56,359 (93rd percentile) is pricing a credit upcycle — but CD ratio at 81.4% with NBFC credit growth accelerating to 14.9% from 10.6% is the setup for a risk-weight circular, not a re-rating; the last time this configuration appeared, NIM compression followed within three quarters. Office REITs tell a different story: Embassy at ₹369.92 and Mindspace at ₹345.06 are both down sharply from prior levels despite Bengaluru rents at a 95th-percentile ₹163/sqft and Mumbai at ₹171/sqft — the market is discounting that absorption at 26.9 mn sqft (21st percentile) cannot sustain rent levels that have decoupled from occupancy fundamentals. Residential real estate launches at 106,000 units (0th percentile) and sales at 90,715 units (0th percentile) with unsold inventory at 616,000 units confirm that the housing cycle is in a digestion phase — home loan rates at 8.35% rising from a trough of 7.4% are doing the work that affordability math predicted. Gold at $4,424/oz (76th percentile) rising four of the last five months is the hedge that sophisticated money is running alongside the equity long.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Three risks, ranked by probability × impact: first, fuel inflation at 22.93% with Brent at $99.29 — every $10 sustained above $90 adds approximately $15bn to India's import bill, widens CAD by 40bps, and feeds back into CPI with a two-quarter lag at a moment when the RBI's own forecast has already jumped to 5.1%. Second, the CD ratio at 81.4% with NBFC credit accelerating — if deposit mobilisation does not close the 630bps gap within two quarters, the regulator's response will be macro-prudential tightening (risk weights, LCR buffers) that hits credit availability faster than a rate hike would. Third, BOJ normalisation at 1.0% combined with US 10Y at 4.94% is structurally repricing EM carry — INR at 96.15 (91st percentile) with India VIX at a complacent 6th percentile means the currency is vulnerable to a disorderly move if FII equity flows reverse again, as they did as recently as two months ago. Watch Thursday's US core PCE print — at 3.34% it remains the single data point most likely to determine whether the Fed's extended pause becomes a tightening, and every basis point of upside surprise lands directly on the INR.
