Executive Summary
▾
01
India Macro Regime

7.8% GDP growth with capacity utilisation at 77.4% — an 18-month high — is the strongest combination India has printed since the post-COVID capex reboot, but the composition demands scrutiny. IIP overall decelerated to 6.7% from 8.8% even as capital goods surged 16.1%, which means the investment cycle is real but the broader industrial base is not yet participating. Two-wheeler sales at 19.7% YoY confirm rural demand is recovering — the informal economy's most reliable barometer — while credit card growth at 2.3% (26th percentile) suggests urban mass-market spending is more measured than the GST headline implies. The growth story is genuine; it is also narrower than the 7.8% print suggests.

02
Global Macro Regime

US GDP at 1.5% SAAR — down from 2.1% — while US PMI composite hits 56.0 (84th percentile) is the central contradiction in global macro: activity surveys are pricing a soft landing that the hard GDP data is already questioning. BOJ at 1.0% (86th percentile) is the structural shift that matters most for EM carry — every 25bps of Japanese normalisation tightens global dollar liquidity by more than an equivalent Fed move, because it unwinds a decade of yen-funded positioning. China's composite PMI at 49.5 with GDP at 4.3% (down from 5.0%) removes the one engine that could have offset US softness — copper at $6.71/lb (80th percentile) is holding, but iron ore's volatility signals the commodity complex is reading China's trajectory with more scepticism than equity markets are.

03
Liquidity Conditions

DII equity net at ₹58,502 crore — a 100th-percentile print, rising for two consecutive months — is absorbing every rupee of FII ambivalence, but the composition of that domestic flow is shifting in a way that matters. SIP AUM at ₹18.62 trillion is growing at 2.3% MoM while SIP accounts grow only 1.1% MoM — average ticket size is rising sharply, which means affluent retail is entering, not mass-market adoption broadening. That is a qualitatively different liquidity source: stickier in normal times, but more correlated with equity market levels and more likely to pause if Nifty corrects 8-10%. US 10Y at 4.94% — easing from 5.01% but still at the 89th percentile — keeps the opportunity cost of EM equity elevated for foreign allocators, and INR at 96.15 adds a currency drag that FII return calculations cannot ignore.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

Bank Nifty at 56,359 (93rd percentile) is pricing a credit cycle that the deposit data does not yet support — with CD ratio at 81.4% and deposit growth at 10.8%, any macro-prudential tightening on risk weights would compress NIMs and trigger earnings downgrades that passive index flows would amplify on the way down. Office real estate is the cleaner story: vacancy compressing to 13.9% from 17.2% with Bengaluru rents up 6.4% to ₹107/sqft confirms the GCC absorption thesis, yet Embassy, Mindspace, and Brookfield REITs are all trading well below prior levels — the physical market and the listed vehicle are pricing the same asset differently, and that gap historically closes upward. Gold at ₹13,632/gram (100th percentile, rising 4 of 5 sessions) is the hedge that domestic capital is quietly building — not against global risk, but against a rupee that a 15bps real rate cannot defend.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Three risks, ranked by probability × impact. First: fuel inflation at 22.93% (95th percentile) with Brent at $99.35 — every $5 move toward $105 adds roughly ₹75,000 crore to the annual import bill, widens CAD by 20bps, and feeds back into CPI with a two-quarter lag that the RBI's 5.1% forecast may not fully capture. Second: BOJ at 1.0% with US 10Y still at the 89th percentile creates the conditions for a carry-trade unwind that would hit INR, EM bonds, and India's ₹58,502 crore DII-supported equity market simultaneously — the domestic bid is large but not infinite. Third: the CD ratio at 81.4% with NBFC credit accelerating to 14.9% sets up a regulatory response risk — the RBI has used risk-weight increases twice in the last cycle to cool unsecured lending, and the incentive to act again is rising. Watch Friday's RBI weekly credit-deposit data for any further widening of the 630bps gap — that is the single number that determines whether the base case holds or the bear scenario begins.

Inflation Cycle
CPI 4.45%; Fuel 22.93%

RBI's own inflation forecast just jumped 50bps to 5.1%; the gap between that forecast and the current repo rate of 5.25% leaves a real rate of 15bps — insufficient to anchor expectations when WPI is running at 9.92%.

NBFC credit growth accelerating to 14.9% from 10.6% signals that wholesale funding channels are being tapped to bridge what retail deposits cannot — a setup that historically precedes risk-weight tightening, not rate cuts.
