Executive Summary
▾
01
India Macro Regime

7.8% GDP growth with PMI composite at 54.6 and IIP capital goods at 16.1% presents a coherent expansion narrative — until you notice that IIP overall has decelerated to 6.7% from 8.8% and core sector to 5.4% from 6.0%, suggesting the industrial base is narrowing even as the investment cycle headline strengthens. Capacity utilisation at 77.4% — up 180bps in a single month — is the most credible forward indicator for private capex commitment, sitting one quarter away from the 78-80% threshold that historically triggers balance-sheet investment decisions. Two-wheeler sales at 19.7% YoY confirm that the informal economy is participating in this expansion, not just the formal corporate sector captured by GST. Credit card growth at 2.3% — a 26th-percentile low, down from 5.6% — is the single data point that does not fit the consumption optimism, and it deserves more attention than it is getting.

02
Global Macro Regime

Euro Stoxx 50 at a 100th-percentile 6,318 and US PMI composite at 56 — an 84th-percentile print, up from 54.5 — are pricing a global growth re-acceleration that US 10Y at 5.01% simultaneously prices as structurally inflationary. US GDP SAAR has decelerated to 1.5% from 2.1%, yet equities are at all-time highs and PMI is expanding — a divergence that resolves either through earnings disappointment or through a bond market that is simply wrong about the terminal rate. China at 4.3% GDP (down from 5.0%) with composite PMI at 49.5 remains the deflationary anchor in the global system, keeping commodity prices from a full breakout despite Brent at $96.22. BOJ at 1.0% — a 25bps hike from 0.75% — is the structural shift that matters most for global carry: every basis point of Japanese rate normalisation tightens global dollar liquidity by unwinding yen-funded positions.

03
Liquidity Conditions

DII equity net at ₹58,502 crore — a 100th-percentile print, rising for two consecutive months from ₹8,930 crore — is the most important domestic liquidity fact of the week, and it is being funded by SIP inflows at a record ₹32,297 crore with SIP AUM crossing ₹18.62 trillion. FII equity net at ₹29,631 crore has swung sharply positive from -₹3,112 crore prior month, but the trend data shows this is a single-month reversal, not a sustained re-entry — the sequence reads -114 → -3,112 → 29,631, which is a bounce, not a regime change. Bank credit at 17.1% against deposits at 10.8% means the banking system is funding incremental credit growth through wholesale markets and CD issuances, not through deposit mobilisation — a structurally fragile arrangement when US 10Y is at 5.01% and global risk appetite can reverse. Corporate bond issuance at ₹18,700 crore is at a 0th-percentile low, suggesting corporates are not yet tapping capital markets to bridge the funding gap — they are relying on bank credit, which tightens the CD ratio further.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

Nifty at 23,414 (75th percentile) and Bank Nifty at 56,471 (93rd percentile) are pricing an earnings cycle that fuel inflation at 22.93% and WPI at 9.92% will test in the next two quarters — margin compression in industrials and consumer staples is the arithmetic consequence, not a tail risk. Office absorption at 29.9 million sqft with vacancy falling to 13.9% from 17.2% tells a structurally positive commercial real estate story, but the REIT prices are telling the opposite: Embassy REIT down to ₹369.92 from ₹441.07, Mindspace to ₹345.06 from ₹505.58 — the market is discounting the asset class against a US 10Y at 5.01% that makes the yield spread unattractive. GCC leasing at 35%+ of total office absorption (vs 15% five years ago) is the structural shift that the REIT derating is ignoring: this is not cyclical IT demand but sovereign-grade corporate infrastructure investment that does not vacate on a hiring freeze. Residential real estate shows unsold inventory at 616,000 units rising while sales at 90,715 units fall — affordability at a 19th-percentile index of 3.5 with home loan rates at 7.85% is the binding constraint.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Ranked by probability × impact: first, fuel inflation at 22.93% feeding into core CPI with a two-quarter lag — if core moves from 3.9% toward 4.5%, the RBI's extended pause becomes a forced hike, repricing every rate-sensitive asset simultaneously. Second, the credit-deposit divergence at 630bps — if deposit growth does not accelerate to 13-14% within two quarters, the RBI's macro-prudential toolkit (risk weights, LCR tightening) becomes the next policy lever, and NBFC credit at 14.9% is the first casualty. Third, US 10Y at 5.01% with Fed funds at 3.63% — the inverted logic of a steepening curve in a slowing economy (US GDP SAAR at 1.5%) suggests either a fiscal premium being priced or a growth re-acceleration; either resolution is disruptive for EM flows. Fourth, INR at 96.15 with Brent flat at $96.22 — any crude spike above $100 widens CAD by 40bps per $10 increment and forces RBI FX intervention that draws down reserves. Watch Thursday's US core PCE print at 3.34% — if it moves above 3.5%, the Fed's pause narrative breaks and every EM carry trade unwinds simultaneously.
