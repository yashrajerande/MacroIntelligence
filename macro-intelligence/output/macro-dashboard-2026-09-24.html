01
India Macro Regime

Consumption Fires on All Cylinders — Margins Pay the Price

The facts:

  • PV, two-wheeler, and CV sales all grew 16–20% YoY simultaneously — a broad-based demand signal last seen in FY22.
  • GST at ₹1,99,853 Cr looks like a record; but YTD at ₹10.42 lakh crore sits at the 14th percentile — advance filings flatter the monthly print.
  • Fuel inflation at 22.93% (95th percentile) feeds into WPI at 9.92% — producer cost pressure that services CPI will absorb within two quarters.
  • But core CPI at 3.9% (28th percentile) remains contained, creating a false sense of comfort at the headline level.

The tension: Demand is real — three vehicle segments and retail mall vacancy at a cycle-low 5% confirm it. But fuel at 22.93% is a margin tax on every business serving that demand; the consumer is present, the producer's profitability is not.

Bottom line: Watch two-wheeler sales as the rural income proxy — a deceleration below 15% would signal the consumption story is narrowing to urban affluent only.

02
Global Macro Regime

DM Surveys Accelerate — Nominal GDP Says Otherwise

The facts:

  • US PMI composite at 56.0 (84th percentile) and Global PMI at 53.5 (82nd percentile) signal re-acceleration.
  • But US GDP SAAR slowed to 1.5% from 2.1%, and China GDP fell to 4.3% from 5.0% with PMI at 49.5 — contraction territory.
  • US 10Y at 4.96% (90th percentile) is doing the Fed's tightening for it; real economy stress is already embedded in the yield curve.
  • BOJ at 1.0% (86th percentile, up from 0.75%) is the underappreciated variable — yen carry unwind accelerates as the rate differential compresses.

The tension: Survey data prices re-acceleration; nominal GDP data prices deceleration. China at 4.3% prevents commodity reflation from sustaining, which is the one scenario that would validate the survey optimism.

Bottom line: BOJ's next move is the tail risk for India liquidity — a further 25bps hike would accelerate FII repatriation from EM into yen-denominated assets.

03
Liquidity Conditions

Domestic Flows at Record Highs — Funded by Thinner Deposit Base

The facts:

  • DII equity net at ₹58,502 Cr (100th percentile, rising two consecutive months) — domestic capital is absorbing every FII wobble.
  • SIP AUM at ₹18.62 Tr growing 40% faster than account growth — average ticket size rising to ₹1.86 lakh signals affluent money deepening, not mass participation widening.
  • But bank deposit growth at 10.8% vs credit at 17.1% — the same pool funding SIP inflows is being stretched to fund loan books simultaneously.
  • FII equity net at ₹29,631 Cr (83rd percentile) is positive, but FII debt flows are likely negative given INR at 96.15 and US 10Y at 4.96%.

The tension: Equity liquidity looks abundant; the underlying deposit base funding both credit growth and mutual fund inflows is not. SIP inflows are a flow, not a stock — a sentiment shift in the affluent segment reverses them faster than CASA deposits.

Bottom line: If SIP YoY growth (currently 14%, down from 16%) falls below 10%, DII absorption capacity shrinks and the market loses its domestic bid.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

Retail Real Estate Tightens — Listed REITs Disagree Loudly

The facts:

  • Retail mall vacancy collapsed to 5.0% (8th percentile, down from 7.5%) — the tightest in the current cycle, driven by quick commerce, D2C, and cloud kitchen tenants.
  • Office absorption at 26.9 Mn sqft (21st percentile) is GCC-led, not traditional IT — Nifty IT flat YTD is the wrong lens for office RE.
  • But Embassy, Mindspace, and Brookfield REITs are down 15–25% YTD — listed proxies pricing a vacancy story the physical market has already resolved.
  • Nifty 50 at 23,447 (75th percentile) and Bank Nifty at 56,549 (93rd percentile) — banking index at near-cycle highs while deposit stress is structurally unresolved.

The tension: Physical real estate — both retail and office — is tightening. Listed REITs are pricing distress. Bank Nifty at 93rd percentile assumes NIM stability that a 630bps credit-deposit divergence will eventually challenge.

Bottom line: REITs at 15–25% discounts to physical market fundamentals are a mispricing worth monitoring — but the catalyst requires FII debt flows to stabilise, which needs INR below 95.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Four Fuses, One Detonator — INR Holds Them All Together

The facts:

  • INR at 96.15 (91st percentile, weakened 90 paise in 5 months) — every further leg down reprices import costs, fuel subsidies, and FII return calculations simultaneously.
  • Fuel inflation at 22.93% (95th percentile) — if Brent sustains above $100, the pass-through to CPI forces RBI's hand on rates regardless of growth signals.
  • CD ratio at 81.4% (75th percentile) with NBFC credit accelerating to 14.9% — the last time this ratio sustained above 80% with a thin real rate, macro-prudential tightening followed within three quarters.
  • India VIX at 10.35 (5th percentile, falling five consecutive months) — complacency at this level historically precedes a volatility spike of 40–60% within 60 days.

The tension: Each risk is manageable in isolation; INR weakness is the transmission mechanism that makes them simultaneous — a depreciating rupee tightens imported inflation, pressures RBI, raises NBFC funding costs, and triggers FII outflows in one chain.

Bottom line: Watch INR/USD — a print above 97.00 this week would force RBI intervention that drains liquidity and reprices the entire rate-cut narrative the equity market is still holding.
