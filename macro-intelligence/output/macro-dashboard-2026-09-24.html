Executive Summary
▾
01
India Macro Regime

7.8% GDP growth with PMI composite at 54.6 looks like a clean expansion — until you separate the engines. Capital goods IIP at 16.1% and capacity utilisation at 77.4% confirm a genuine investment upcycle; but IIP overall at 6.7% (down from 8.8%) and core sector at 5.4% (down from 6%) signal that the broader industrial base is not accelerating in lockstep. Fuel inflation at 22.93% — a 10-year high — is the variable that can unravel the narrative fastest: every sustained quarter at these levels compresses rural disposable income, and two-wheeler sales at 19.7%, while strong today, are the first indicator to watch for demand fatigue. RBI's own growth forecast at 6.7% sits 110bps below the current print — the gap between what the central bank believes is sustainable and what the data currently shows is itself a signal worth holding.

02
Global Macro Regime

US PMI composite at 56.0 (84th percentile) and Euro Stoxx 50 at a 100th-percentile 6,299 — even after today's marginal pullback — describe a global economy that is re-accelerating, which is precisely the wrong backdrop for central banks trying to declare victory on inflation. US 10Y at 4.96% (90th percentile) with Fed funds flat at 3.63% means the bond market is doing the Fed's tightening for it — but US GDP SAAR at 1.5% (down from 2.1%) says the real economy is already feeling it. BOJ at 1.0% (86th percentile, up from 0.75%) is the underappreciated global variable: yen carry unwind at this pace historically correlates with EM volatility within 60-90 days, and India's INR at 96.15 is already at the 91st percentile. China at 4.3% GDP (down from 5.0%) with composite PMI at 49.5 — below 50 — is the demand drag that prevents any commodity-led global reflation from becoming self-sustaining.

03
Liquidity Conditions

DII equity net at ₹58,502 Cr — a 100th-percentile all-time high, rising for two consecutive months — is the most consequential liquidity fact of the week, and it is being driven by SIP inflows at ₹32,297 Cr (92nd percentile) where AUM at ₹18.62 Tr is growing faster than account additions — average ticket size is rising, meaning affluent savers are increasing allocation, not just new participants entering. FII equity net swung to ₹29,631 Cr positive from -₹3,112 Cr prior month, but the trend is volatile (1 of last 2 months positive) — this is not a structural FII return, it is a positioning adjustment. The risk embedded in this liquidity picture: India VIX at 10.345 (5th percentile, falling for five consecutive sessions) signals a market pricing near-zero tail risk precisely when BOJ tightening, INR at decade highs, and fuel inflation at decade highs are all live variables — complacency at this level has historically preceded sharp, fast corrections rather than gradual ones.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

Nifty 50 at 23,447 (75th percentile) and Bank Nifty at 56,549 (93rd percentile) are pricing a credit cycle that the deposit data does not yet support — Bank Nifty at the 93rd percentile while CD ratio sits at 81.4% and deposit growth trails credit by 630bps is a divergence that resolves either through NIM compression or macro-prudential tightening, neither of which is in current consensus earnings. Office absorption at 24.6 Mn sqft rising 18% sequentially tells a structurally different story from listed REIT prices: Embassy, Mindspace, and Brookfield REITs are down 15-25% from prior levels even as GCC leasing — now 35-40% of total office demand versus 15% five years ago — is structurally repricing the demand base. Residential real estate shows unsold inventory at 6,16,000 units (62nd percentile, rising) alongside declining sales units — affordability at the 19th percentile with home loan rates averaging 7.55% is the binding constraint, and any further INR weakness feeding into imported inflation will delay the rate relief that housing demand is waiting for.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Three risks, ranked by probability × impact. First: fuel inflation at 22.93% with Brent at $97.81 — if Brent crosses $100 and sustains, every $10 adds approximately $15bn to India's import bill, widens CAD by 40bps, and puts the RBI in the position of defending INR with reserves rather than rate policy, since hiking into a slowing IIP is not viable. Second: BOJ at 1.0% unwinding yen carry — the historical window for EM stress after a BOJ hike of this magnitude is 60-90 days; India's FX reserves provide a buffer, but INR at the 91st percentile with VIX at the 5th percentile means the market is not priced for the test. Third: bank credit at 17.1% against deposit growth of 10.8% — if NBFC wholesale funding costs rise as the carry unwind pressures domestic rates, the credit channel tightens precisely when the capex cycle needs it most open. Watch US 10Y — a sustained break above 5.0% this week is the single data point that tightens all three risks simultaneously.

Growth Cycle

PMI composite at 54.6 is encouraging, but manufacturing PMI slipped to 52.8 from 53.5 — the services sector is doing the heavy lifting, and services-led growth at this stage of the cycle tends to be less employment-generative in the informal economy where 85% of workers sit.
Slowing Pace
Inflation Cycle
CPI 4.45%; Fuel 22.93%
CPI at 4.45% masks a dangerous internal split: core CPI at 3.9% (28th percentile, genuinely subdued) while fuel inflation at 22.93% sits at a 10-year high — the 95th percentile. This divergence is the RBI's trap: core gives it cover to hold, but fuel at these levels feeds into transport costs, logistics margins, and eventually food prices with a 2-quarter lag. WPI at 9.92% rising alongside fuel means producer margins are being squeezed — either they absorb it (earnings risk) or they pass it through (CPI risk in Q1 FY28).
Inflation Overshoot
Credit Cycle
Credit 17.1%; CD 81.4%
Bank credit at 17.1% YoY against deposit growth of 10.8% — a 630bps divergence — with the CD ratio at 81.4% (75th percentile) is the structural fault line of this cycle. NBFC credit growth accelerating to 14.9% from 10.6% signals that as banks tighten deposit competition, NBFCs are filling the gap via wholesale funding — precisely the channel that seized in 2018. Credit card growth at 2.3% (down from 5.6%) suggests the regulator's risk-weight tightening on unsecured lending is already biting at the margin; the question is whether that restraint spreads to secured categories before deposit growth catches up.
Deposit Gap Stress
Policy Regime
Repo 5.25%
Repo at 5.25% with RBI's own inflation forecast revised up 50bps to 5.1% leaves a real rate of approximately 15bps — thin enough to be irrelevant as an anchor. With INR at 96.15 (91st percentile) and fuel inflation at a decade high, the RBI is not cutting; but it cannot hike without choking a capex cycle that capacity utilisation data says is just beginning. Extended pause is the only viable stance, but every month of pause with credit growing at 17% and deposits at 10.8% is a month the banking system's structural vulnerability deepens.

Capex Cycle
IIP CapG 16.1%; CU 77.4%

Core sector at 5.4% (moderating from 6%) is the caveat: cement and steel dispatch, the ground-level proxies for actual construction activity, are decelerating even as the headline investment numbers look strong. Manufacturing PMI at 52.8 (down from 53.5) confirms order book stability without overheating — a Goldilocks setup for capex, provided the credit channel doesn't tighten before private investment fully takes the baton from public capex.
