India Macro Regime

Services Carry Growth — Goods Economy Quietly Fades

The facts:

  • PMI Composite at 54.6 (83rd percentile) — but PMI Manufacturing fell to 52.8 from 53.5 while Services rose to 54.5 from 53.3; goods momentum is reversing.
  • IIP YoY slowed to 6.7% YoY from 8.8%; core sector eased to 5.4% YoY from 6.0% — upstream industrial signals contradict the composite headline.
  • Capacity utilisation at 77.4% of capacity (74th percentile, up from 75.6%) is the one unambiguous positive — above the threshold where private capex becomes self-reinforcing.
  • But two-wheeler sales at 19.7% YoY and PV sales at 16% YoY suggest consumption is holding — the question is whether it is demand or credit-pull.

The tension: GDP at 7.8% YoY is accelerating on paper; the goods economy — IIP, core sector, manufacturing PMI — is decelerating in practice. Services and consumption are carrying a load that industrial output is not confirming.

Bottom line: Watch core sector YoY in the next print: a third consecutive deceleration below 5% YoY would signal the goods economy is diverging structurally from the headline.

Global Macro Regime

Equity All-Time Highs — Bond Market Prices the Reckoning

The facts:

  • Nikkei 225 at 66,364 (100th percentile) and Euro Stoxx 50 at 6,303 (100th percentile) — both at all-time highs, pricing synchronised global expansion.
  • But US 10Y Treasury at 5.18% yield (94th percentile, up from 5.11%) is tightening real financial conditions faster than any central bank rate decision.
  • US GDP SAAR slowed to 1.5% SAAR from 2.1%; China GDP at 4.3% YoY from 5.0% with PMI Composite at 49.5 — the two largest economies are decelerating.
  • BOJ rate at 1.0% p.a. (86th percentile, highest in 10 years) — yen carry unwind is the underappreciated transmission mechanism into EM equity flows.

The tension: Global equities are at all-time highs; global growth is slowing and bond yields are near decade highs. One of these is wrong — and the bond market has a better forecasting record.

Bottom line: BOJ at 1.0% p.a. is the tail risk for India liquidity: any further hike accelerates yen carry unwind and pressures FII equity flows, which are already volatile (₹-114 cr → ₹29,631 cr net / month in three months).

Liquidity Conditions

Domestic Flows at Record — Deposit Gap Caps the Ceiling

The facts:

  • DII equity net at ₹58,502 cr net / month (100th percentile, all-time high) — domestic institutional absorption is at a level that has historically cushioned FII volatility.
  • SIP inflows at ₹32,297 cr / month (92nd percentile, rising two consecutive months); SIP AUM at ₹18.62 lakh cr (93rd percentile).
  • But bank credit at 17.1% YoY vs deposit growth at 10.8% YoY — a 630bps gap — keeps the CD ratio at 81.4% credit ÷ deposits (75th percentile).
  • Corporate bond issuance at ₹18,700 cr / month (0th percentile — lowest on record) means the capital market relief valve is closed; NBFC credit accelerating to 14.9% YoY from 10.6% signals wholesale funding migration.

The tension: Equity liquidity is at all-time highs; credit liquidity is structurally strained. The two pools are not connected — strong SIP flows do not fund bank deposits, and the deposit gap is not narrowing.

Bottom line: If deposit growth does not recover toward 13% YoY within two quarters, macro-prudential tightening via risk weights on unsecured lending becomes the base case, not the tail risk.

Equity + Real Estate Implications

NRI Demand Holds Premium — Office REITs Price the Absorption Collapse

The facts:

  • NRI buying is RISING — NRI share of residential purchases at 20% (up from 15%, +5 pp), with NRI share within premium/luxury at 25%; remittances at $135.46 bn (14% YoY) sustain the bid.
  • Launch mix skewed to premium and above: luxury (₹1.5–2.5 cr) at 25% of supply and ultra-luxury (>₹2.5 cr) at 22% — but sales momentum is negative in MMR (-8% YoY), NCR (-6% YoY), and Pune (-15% YoY).
  • Office absorption at 13.2 mn sq ft / quarter (6th percentile) — GCC occupiers at 42% of leasing are holding rents stable (Bengaluru ₹100/sqft/mo, Mumbai ₹126/sqft/mo) even as aggregate volumes collapse.
  • But Embassy REIT at ₹370/unit, Mindspace at ₹345/unit, and Brookfield at ₹263/unit are all down sharply from prior prints — the equity market is pricing the absorption weakness, not the rent stability.
  • Bank Nifty at 55,580 (92nd percentile) — near all-time highs despite the CD ratio stress — is the equity market's most visible complacency signal.

The tension: NRI demand and GCC leasing are holding the premium and office rent floors; but residential sales volumes are falling in five of seven cities and REIT unit prices are declining. The floor is real; the ceiling is not.

Bottom line: Overweight premium residential in NRI-favoured cities (MMR, Bengaluru, Hyderabad) on NRI bid; avoid office REITs until absorption recovers above 20 mn sq ft / quarter.

Key Risks to Monitor

Three Fuses, One Detonator — Sequencing Is Everything

The facts:

  • Fuel inflation at 22.93% YoY (95th percentile) — if Brent holds above $95/bbl, WPI-to-CPI passthrough forces RBI's inflation forecast above 5.5% YoY, closing the rate-cut window entirely.
  • US 10Y at 5.18% yield (94th percentile, rising) — every 25bps further tightens global financial conditions, pressures INR at 96.15 (91st percentile), and risks FII reversal from the current ₹29,631 cr net / month.
  • CD ratio at 81.4% credit ÷ deposits with corporate bond issuance at a record-low ₹18,700 cr / month — if deposit growth does not recover, macro-prudential tightening on NBFC risk weights is the regulatory response.
  • India VIX at 12.16 (7th percentile, falling five consecutive sessions) — historically, sub-12 VIX precedes sharp corrections; complacency is priced in at precisely the moment global tail risks are rising.

The tension: Each risk is manageable in isolation; the danger is sequencing — a US 10Y spike triggers INR weakness, which feeds fuel inflation, which forces RBI tightening, which hits the CD ratio stress simultaneously.

Bottom line: Watch US 10Y Treasury this week: a close above 5.25% yield would reprice India rate-cut expectations, pressure INR, and likely trigger the first meaningful FII equity outflow since July.

NRI Bid Rising — Top-End Supply Building

The facts:

  • Launch mix (supply): 47% of new launches priced above ₹1.5 cr; affordable just 6% — sales split by band not published this print
  • City momentum: Kolkata leads at 10% sales YoY; Pune trails at -15%
  • NRI buying is RISING: 20% of residential purchases (+5 pp vs prior period in source); 25% within premium/luxury; concentrated in Mumbai Metropolitan Region, Delhi-NCR, Bengaluru
  • Office: Bengaluru leads leasing at 5.59 mn sq ft / quarter (8% YoY); GCCs take 42% of leasing

The tension: NRI demand is rising and developers are launching 47% of new supply above ₹1.5 cr — both sides are crowding into the top end, and whether sales absorb that supply is the number this print does not publish.

Bottom line: Watch the sales-share split by band next quarter: if luxury sales share prints below its 47% launch share, unsold inventory builds at the top end first, NRI bid or not.
