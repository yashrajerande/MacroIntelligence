India Macro Regime

Services Carry GDP While Goods Economy Loses Altitude

The facts:

  • India GDP at 7.8% YoY — but IIP fell to 6.7% YoY from 8.8% YoY and core sector slipped to 5.4% YoY from 6.0% YoY; goods-economy proxies are decelerating.
  • PMI Composite at 54.6 (83rd percentile) is services-led: PMI Services rose to 54.5 while PMI Manufacturing eased to 52.8 from 53.5.
  • GST at ₹1,99,853 cr / month — up from ₹1,74,116 cr / month — is a 14.8% sequential jump; advance filing effects likely explain a portion of the beat.
  • But two-wheeler sales at 19.7% YoY and PV sales at 16% YoY confirm genuine mass-market demand, even as credit card growth collapses to 2.3% YoY from 5.6% YoY.

The tension: GDP at 7.8% YoY and GST at a record print suggest broad expansion; IIP deceleration and credit card collapse suggest the goods economy and mass-market credit are already softening beneath the headline.

Bottom line: Watch IIP capital goods for a second consecutive deceleration — if it prints below 12% YoY next month, the capex narrative loses its primary evidence.

Global Macro Regime

Euro Stoxx at All-Time Highs — Growth Data Says Otherwise

The facts:

  • Euro Stoxx 50 at 6,269 (100th percentile) — pricing perfection — while Eurozone GDP is only 1.2% YoY and Eurozone CPI re-accelerated to 3.3% YoY from 2.9% YoY.
  • US PMI Composite at 56.0 (84th percentile) diverges from US GDP SAAR slowing to 2.2% SAAR from 2.5% SAAR; surveys and output are telling different stories.
  • US 10Y Treasury at 5.26% yield (95th percentile) with Fed Funds at 3.63% p.a. — a 163bps inversion — means bond markets are tightening conditions the Fed has not formally endorsed.
  • But China PMI Composite at 49.5 (contraction) with GDP at 4.3% YoY from 5.0% YoY removes the commodity-reflation backstop that DM equity bulls are implicitly assuming.

The tension: DM equity indices are at or near all-time highs; underlying nominal growth is decelerating in both the US and China, and real tightening is already embedded in the 10Y yield. The surveys are the trapdoor.

Bottom line: Hedge DM equity exposure: if US 10Y breaches 5.35% yield, the equity risk premium compression that drove the S&P 500 to 7,652 reverses mechanically.

Liquidity Conditions

Domestic Flows at Record — But Funded by Shrinking Deposit Base

The facts:

  • DII equity net at ₹58,502 cr / month (100th percentile, rising 2 in a row) and SIP inflows at ₹32,297 cr / month (92nd percentile) are providing a structural bid to Indian equities.
  • MF AUM at ₹87.08 lakh cr (86th percentile) with SIP AUM at ₹18.62 lakh cr (93rd percentile) — the affluent household is systematically rotating from deposits to mutual funds.
  • But deposit growth at 10.8% YoY against bank credit at 17.1% YoY means the same household rotation that funds SIP inflows is draining the deposit base banks need to lend.
  • FII equity net at ₹29,631 cr / month (83rd percentile) has reversed — but US 10Y at 5.26% yield makes this reversal fragile; carry economics deteriorate above 5.25% yield.

The tension: Record domestic flows into equities and the deposit-to-MF rotation are two sides of the same coin: the liquidity that looks strong in equity markets is the liquidity that is missing from bank balance sheets.

Bottom line: Monitor FII flows weekly — a reversal back to net selling above ₹15,000 cr / month outflow would expose the market to a DII absorption test at current SIP run-rates.

Bottom line: Avoid office REITs until office vacancy stabilises below 14% vacant nationally — at 16% vacant and rising, the yield compression thesis requires a rate cut that is not coming.

Key Risks to Monitor

Three Fuses, One Detonator — US Yields Hold the Match

The facts:

  • US 10Y at 5.26% yield (95th percentile, rising 3 of last 5 observations) — if sustained above 5.25% yield, EM carry unwinds accelerate and FII equity flows reverse from the current ₹29,631 cr / month net inflow.
  • Brent at $98.35/bbl trending toward $100/bbl — every $10 adds ~$15 bn to India's annual import bill, widens CAD by ~40 bps, and feeds back into CPI with a two-quarter lag; fuel inflation already at 22.93% YoY (95th percentile).
  • CD ratio at 81.4% credit ÷ deposits (75th percentile) with credit growing at 17.1% YoY vs deposits at 10.8% YoY — NBFC wholesale funding stress is the second-order risk if global rates stay elevated.
  • But INR at 96.15 (91st percentile) with RBI defending the 96 level — FX reserve adequacy is the buffer; a break above 97 would signal reserve depletion is accelerating.

The tension: All three domestic risks — oil, credit stress, and INR — have a single external trigger: US 10Y yields. A move above 5.35% yield tightens all three simultaneously, and India has no policy rate room to respond.

Bottom line: Watch US 10Y yield this week — a close above 5.35% yield is the threshold that forces RBI FX intervention, pressures NBFC funding costs, and tests DII absorption capacity in a single session.

NRI Bid Rising — Top-End Supply Building

The facts:

  • Launch mix (supply): 35% of new launches priced above ₹1.5 cr; affordable just 14% — sales split by band not published this print
  • City momentum: Hyderabad leads at 15% sales YoY; Chennai trails at -10%
  • NRI buying is RISING: 20% of residential purchases (+5 pp vs prior period in source); 25% within premium/luxury; concentrated in Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad
  • Office: Bengaluru leads leasing at 5.2 mn sq ft / quarter (11% YoY); GCCs take 42% of leasing

The tension: NRI demand is rising and developers are launching 35% of new supply above ₹1.5 cr — both sides are crowding into the top end, and whether sales absorb that supply is the number this print does not publish.

Bottom line: Watch the sales-share split by band next quarter: if luxury sales share prints below its 35% launch share, unsold inventory builds at the top end first, NRI bid or not.
