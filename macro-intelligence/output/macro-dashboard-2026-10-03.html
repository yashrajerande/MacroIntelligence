01
India Macro Regime

Leveraged Growth — Credit Funding What Deposits Cannot

The facts:

  • India GDP at 7.8% YoY with PMI Composite at 54.6, but IIP overall slowed to 6.7% YoY from 8.8% and core sector to 5.4% YoY from 6.0% — the goods economy is decelerating beneath the headline.
  • Capacity utilisation at 77.4% of capacity (74th percentile) supports the capex narrative; credit to industry at 20% YoY and services at 22.9% YoY confirm financing is flowing.
  • But deposit growth at 10.8% YoY against bank credit at 17.1% YoY means the investment cycle is being funded by a widening liability gap, not organic savings mobilisation.
  • Fuel inflation at 22.93% YoY (95th percentile) with RBI's revised forecast at 5.1% YoY closes the rate-cut door — growth is accelerating into a tightening inflation constraint.

The tension: GDP and PMI signal expansion; IIP, core sector, and the credit-deposit gap signal that the expansion is narrowing in quality and increasingly leveraged. The headline and the plumbing disagree.

Bottom line: Watch core sector YoY — a third consecutive monthly deceleration below 5% YoY would signal the capex upcycle is losing its physical foundation.

02
Global Macro Regime

Nikkei at Peak, Bond Market at Pressure — Something Gives

The facts:

  • Nikkei 225 at 68,309 (100th percentile, all-time high) and Euro Stoxx 50 at 6,239 (100th percentile) — DM equities pricing perpetual expansion.
  • But US 10Y Treasury at 5.24% yield (95th percentile) with Fed Funds at 3.75% p.a. means the bond market is tightening financial conditions independently of the Fed.
  • BOJ rate at 1.0% p.a. (86th percentile, up from 0.75%) is unwinding the yen carry trade that funded much of the Nikkei's ascent — the source of the rally is now the source of the risk.
  • China PMI Composite at 49.5 (contraction) with GDP at 4.3% YoY (down from 5.0%) removes the commodity reflation backstop that would otherwise validate equity optimism.

The tension: DM equity indices are at or near all-time highs while bond yields price sustained tightness and China's demand engine stalls. Equities are pricing the bull case; bonds and China are pricing the bear.

Bottom line: BOJ at 1.0% p.a. is the underappreciated tail risk — any further hike accelerates yen carry unwind and hits EM liquidity flows within 60 days.

03
Liquidity Conditions

Domestic Flows at Record Highs, Foreign Flows Structurally Fragile

The facts:

  • DII equity net flows at ₹58,502 cr net / month (100th percentile, all-time high), driven by SIP inflows at ₹32,297 cr / month (92nd percentile) rising for two consecutive months.
  • SIP AUM at ₹18.62 lakh cr (93rd percentile) and MF AUM at ₹87.08 lakh cr (86th percentile) — the domestic bid is structurally deep.
  • But FII equity net flows at ₹29,631 cr net / month swung from -₹3,112 cr net / month the prior month — a reversal driven by positioning, not conviction, given DXY rising four of the last five months to 101.9.
  • India VIX at 14.5 (10th percentile, rising four of five sessions) signals the market is beginning to price risk it has been ignoring.

The tension: Domestic liquidity is at record depth and provides a genuine floor; foreign flows are volatile and DXY-sensitive. The floor holds until SIP growth rate — currently decelerating to 14% YoY from 16% — reverses.

Bottom line: Monitor SIP YoY growth rate: a print below 12% YoY would signal retail participation is plateauing and the domestic bid weakens structurally.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

Nifty Falling Five Straight, REITs Discounting What Rents Are Not

The facts:

  • NRI buying is RISING — NRI share of residential purchases at 20% (up from 15%, +5 pp), with NRI share within premium/luxury at 25%; remittances at $73 bn (12.8% YoY) provide the funding base.
  • Supply skewed to premium and luxury: 34% of launches in the ₹80 lakh–₹1.5 cr band and 24% in ₹1.5–2.5 cr — developers are building for the NRI and affluent domestic buyer, not the mass market.
  • Nifty 50 at 22,422 has fallen five consecutive sessions from 23,447 — the longest losing streak this cycle — while India VIX at 14.5 is at a six-month high.
  • But Embassy REIT at ₹370/unit, Mindspace at ₹345/unit, and Brookfield at ₹263/unit are all down sharply from prior prints despite Bengaluru office rents at ₹163/sqft/mo (95th percentile) and Mumbai at ₹335/sqft/mo (all-time high).

The tension: Office rents at all-time highs while REIT unit prices fall — the market is discounting either absorption risk (at a 14th-percentile 21 mn sq ft / quarter) or the US 10Y at 5.24% making REIT yields uncompetitive. Both are probably true.

Bottom line: Avoid listed REITs until US 10Y yields compress below 4.75% yield or office absorption crosses 25 mn sq ft / quarter — whichever comes first.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Four Fuses, One Detonator — Brent Is the Common Thread

The facts:

  • Fuel inflation at 22.93% YoY (95th percentile) with Brent at $102.7/bbl rising four of five months — every $10 adds ~$15 bn to India's import bill, widens CAD by ~40 bps, and pressures INR.
  • INR at 96.15 (91st percentile of weakness) with DXY at 101.9 rising four of five months — RBI intervention is stabilising but not reversing the trend; reserves are the buffer, not a solution.
  • CD ratio at 81.4% credit ÷ deposits (75th percentile) with NBFC credit growth accelerating to 14.9% YoY — a macro-prudential circular on risk weights would reprice NBFC funding costs overnight.
  • BOJ at 1.0% p.a. (86th percentile) — any further hike triggers yen carry unwind, hits EM flows, and compounds FII volatility already swinging from -₹3,112 cr to +₹29,631 cr net / month in a single month.

The tension: Brent is the common thread across all four risks: it drives fuel inflation, widens CAD, pressures INR, and raises the probability that RBI stays on hold longer — which itself delays the rate relief that would ease NBFC funding stress.

Bottom line: Watch Brent this week: a sustained print above $105/bbl forces RBI to choose between defending INR and supporting growth — and that choice will reprice the entire rate curve.

  • Launch mix (supply): 35% of new launches priced above ₹1.5 cr; affordable just 14% — sales split by band not published this print
  • City momentum: Hyderabad leads at 15% sales YoY; Chennai trails at -10%

  • Office: Bengaluru leads leasing at 5.2 mn sq ft / quarter (11% YoY); GCCs take 42% of leasing

The tension: NRI demand is rising and developers are launching 35% of new supply above ₹1.5 cr — both sides are crowding into the top end, and whether sales absorb that supply is the number this print does not publish.
