India Macro Regime

Services Carry Growth While Manufacturing Loses Altitude

The facts:

  • PMI Composite at 54.6 (83rd percentile) is services-driven at 54.5 — manufacturing PMI fell to 52.8 from 53.5, its third consecutive monthly decline.
  • IIP overall at 6.7% YoY — down from 8.8% — while core sector slipped to 5.4% YoY from 6.0%; upstream inputs are not keeping pace with capital goods orders.
  • Capacity utilisation at 77.4% of capacity (74th percentile, up from 75.6%) is the one number that justifies the capex narrative — above the threshold where private investment historically follows.
  • But two-wheeler sales at 19.7% YoY and PV sales at 16% YoY signal rural and mass-market demand is holding, even as airline passengers at 86.4 mn / month sit at only the 35th percentile.

The tension: The headline GDP at 7.8% YoY is services-led and urban-skewed; manufacturing is losing momentum precisely as the capex cycle needs industrial output to validate it. A services economy cannot sustain 77% capacity utilisation in goods sectors.

Bottom line: Watch manufacturing PMI — a print below 52 would signal the capex cycle is running on financing momentum, not real demand.

02
Global Macro Regime

US Resilience, China Weakness — India Caught Between Both

The facts:

  • US PMI Composite at 56 (84th percentile) and US GDP SAAR rising to 2.2% SAAR from 1.5% — US growth is re-accelerating, which keeps the Fed on hold and the dollar bid.
  • But China GDP at 4.3% YoY (down from 5.0%) with PMI at 49.5 (contraction) removes the commodity reflation tailwind India's exporters were counting on.
  • US 10Y Treasury at 5.24% yield (95th percentile) — bond markets are tightening for the Fed; every 25 bps of yield rise pulls $1-2 bn of EM portfolio flows toward US fixed income.
  • BOJ at 1.0% p.a. (86th percentile, up from 0.75%) is the underappreciated tail: yen carry unwind is not priced into India's FII flow assumptions.

The tension: US resilience keeps global rates high and the dollar strong; China weakness removes the export and commodity demand buffer. India faces the worst combination — imported inflation from oil and imported tightening from US yields — with no offsetting global growth dividend.

Bottom line: If US 10Y holds above 5.25% yield through October, expect FII equity flows to turn negative within 4-6 weeks — watch the weekly FII data.

Liquidity Conditions

Domestic Flows at Record Highs — But the Funding Gap Widens

The facts:

  • DII equity net flows at ₹58,502 cr net / month (100th percentile, rising two consecutive months) — the highest in the 10-year dataset; SIP inflows at ₹32,297 cr / month (92nd percentile).
  • SIP AUM at ₹18.62 lakh cr (93rd percentile) is growing faster than account count at 100.18 mn (82nd percentile) — average ticket size rising to ₹18,600 per account signals HNI/UHNI entry, not mass-market deepening.
  • But bank CD ratio at 81.4% credit ÷ deposits (75th percentile) with credit at 17.1% YoY vs deposits at 10.8% YoY means the banking system is funding growth through costlier wholesale channels.
  • FII equity net at ₹29,631 cr net / month (83rd percentile) looks strong — but two months ago it was negative ₹3,112 cr net / month; the reversal is fragile and BOJ-sensitive.

The tension: Mutual fund flows are at record levels, but they are concentrated in affluent retail — not the broad-based SIP deepening that would make domestic liquidity structurally resilient. Meanwhile, the banking system's funding stress is worsening regardless of equity market inflows.

Bottom line: SIP YoY growth at 14% YoY (down from 16%) is the early warning — if it falls below 12% YoY, the HNI-driven SIP surge is masking underlying retail fatigue.

Key Risks to Monitor

Three Fuses, One Match — Oil, Rates, and the Deposit Gap

The facts:

  • Fuel inflation at 22.93% YoY (95th percentile) with Brent at $102.25/bbl — every $10/bbl adds ~$15 bn to India's annual import bill, widens CAD by ~40 bps, and pushes INR toward 97-98.
  • US 10Y at 5.24% yield (95th percentile) — sustained above 5.25% yield triggers EM carry unwind; FII flows flipped negative as recently as two months ago at -₹3,112 cr net / month.
  • CD ratio at 81.4% credit ÷ deposits with NBFC credit accelerating to 14.9% YoY — wholesale-funded NBFCs face margin compression; a liquidity event in one mid-sized NBFC could reprice the entire sector.
  • But India VIX at 14.46 (10th percentile) remains historically low — options are cheap, which means the market is not yet hedging the tail.

The tension: All three risks — oil, global rates, and domestic funding stress — are individually manageable; the danger is their interaction. Oil widens CAD, which weakens INR, which accelerates FII outflows, which tightens domestic liquidity, which stresses NBFC funding. The chain is short.

Bottom line: Watch Brent crude this week — a close above $105/bbl triggers the full CAD-INR-FII feedback loop and forces RBI's hand on FX intervention.

Slowing Pace
Inflation Cycle
CPI 4.45% YoY; Fuel 22.93% YoY
The headline CPI at 4.45% YoY is almost irrelevant as an analytical object — the real story is the 530 bps gap between fuel inflation at 22.93% YoY (95th percentile) and core CPI at 3.9% YoY (28th percentile). Core this low tells you domestic demand pricing power is absent; fuel this high tells you the import bill is compounding. The second-order effect is the one the market is underpricing: Brent at $102.25/bbl feeds into WPI at 9.92% YoY, which feeds into producer margins, which feeds into either corporate earnings compression or a second-round CPI pass-through in 2-3 quarters. RBI's own inflation forecast has already moved to 5.1% YoY — the repo rate at 5.25% p.a. is barely positive in real terms and offers no buffer if food inflation at 5.52% YoY proves sticky.
Inflation Overshoot
Credit Cycle
Credit 17.1% YoY; CD ratio 81.4%
Bank credit at 17.1% YoY against deposit growth at 10.8% YoY is a 630 bps divergence — the CD ratio at 81.4% credit ÷ deposits sits at the 75th percentile of the 10-year range. The last time this ratio sustained above 80% while the repo rate was being held low, the system resolved it through either deposit rate wars that compressed NIMs or a regulatory intervention on risk weights. NBFC credit growth accelerating to 14.9% YoY from 10.6% YoY looks like momentum; it is actually a sign that wholesale-funded lenders are filling the gap that deposit-constrained banks are leaving — at higher funding costs. The incentive structure here is misaligned: banks grow credit for fee income and market share, management guidance talks granular deposits, but CASA ratios are under pressure. Watch for macro-prudential tightening.

Policy Regime
Repo 5.25% p.a.

The RBI cannot cut while fuel inflation is at the 95th percentile and food inflation is sticky at 5.52% YoY; it cannot hike without choking a capex cycle that is only now gaining traction. The INR at 96.15 adds a third constraint: any signal of dovishness accelerates carry-trade unwind and forces RBI to deploy reserves. The policy rate is frozen, but the effective tightening is happening through the CD ratio and wholesale funding costs — the transmission is working, just not through the channel the market is watching.

The facts:

  • Launch mix (supply): 35% of new launches priced above ₹1.5 cr; affordable just 14% — sales split by band not published this print
  • City momentum: Hyderabad leads at 15% sales YoY; Chennai trails at -10%
  • NRI buying is RISING: 20% of residential purchases (+5 pp vs prior period in source); 25% within premium/luxury; concentrated in Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad
  • Office: Bengaluru leads leasing at 5.2 mn sq ft / quarter (11% YoY); GCCs take 42% of leasing

The tension: NRI demand is rising and developers are launching 35% of new supply above ₹1.5 cr — both sides are crowding into the top end, and whether sales absorb that supply is the number this print does not publish.

Bottom line: Watch the sales-share split by band next quarter: if luxury sales share prints below its 35% launch share, unsold inventory builds at the top end first, NRI bid or not.
