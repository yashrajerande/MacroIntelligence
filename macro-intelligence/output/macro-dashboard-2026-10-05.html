India Macro Regime

Capex Upcycle Meets Inflation Ceiling — RBI Caught in the Middle

The facts:

  • IIP Capital Goods at 16.1% YoY and capacity utilisation at 77.4% of capacity — approaching the 78–80% threshold that historically triggers fresh private investment commitments.
  • But fuel inflation at 22.93% YoY (95th percentile) has forced RBI's own forecast up to 5.1% YoY from 4.6% YoY, closing the door on any near-term rate relief.
  • PMI Composite at 54.6 masks manufacturing's slide to 52.8 — services are carrying the expansion; one soft services print ends the story.
  • Two-wheeler sales at 19.7% YoY and PV sales at 16% YoY confirm consumption is holding across urban and semi-urban segments.

The tension: The capex cycle needs rate stability or easing to sustain private investment IRRs; fuel inflation at a decade-high percentile makes easing impossible. Growth is accelerating into a policy ceiling it cannot break through.

Bottom line: Watch core sector YoY for two consecutive months above 6.0% — that is the confirmation that capex is pulling upstream inputs, not just being financed on paper.

02
Global Macro Regime

US Surveys Signal Strength; Bond Market and China Signal Otherwise

The facts:

  • US PMI Composite at 56 (84th percentile) and Euro Stoxx 50 at 6,239 (100th percentile) price a DM re-acceleration.
  • But US GDP slowed to 2.2% SAAR from 2.5% SAAR, and China GDP decelerated to 4.3% YoY from 5.0% YoY with PMI Composite at 49.5 — in contraction.
  • US 10Y Treasury at 5.24% yield (95th percentile) is doing the Fed's tightening work; real economy stress is already embedded in the curve.
  • BOJ at 1.0% p.a. (86th percentile, up from 0.75% p.a.) is the underappreciated tail — yen carry unwind accelerates as the rate differential compresses.

The tension: DM equity markets are pricing survey-based optimism; nominal GDP deceleration in the US and outright contraction signals in China suggest the surveys are a leading indicator of nothing — or of a soft landing that the bond market has already rejected.

Bottom line: BOJ's next move is the global liquidity trigger to watch — a further hike to 1.25% p.a. would accelerate yen carry unwind and pressure EM flows including India within 60 days.

03
Liquidity Conditions

Domestic Flows at Record Highs; Funding Plumbing Under Stress

The facts:

  • SIP inflows at ₹32,297 cr / month (92nd percentile, rising two consecutive months) and DII equity net at ₹58,502 cr net / month (100th percentile) represent the strongest domestic absorption on record.
  • FII equity net at ₹29,631 cr net / month is positive but volatile — the prior two months were ₹-114 cr and ₹-3,112 cr net / month respectively.
  • But corporate bond issuance at ₹18,700 cr / month (lowest historical decile) signals capital markets are not providing an alternative funding channel as bank deposits lag.
  • MF AUM at ₹87.08 lakh cr (86th percentile) is a stock of savings seeking equity returns — but it is not solving the banking system's deposit shortfall.

The tension: Retail savings are flowing into mutual funds at record pace, bypassing the banking system — which is precisely what widens the credit-deposit gap and forces banks toward wholesale funding at higher cost.

Bottom line: Watch deposit growth: a sustained print below 11% YoY for two more months will force RBI's hand on macro-prudential tightening, regardless of the rate stance.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

Retail Real Estate Tightens; Office REITs Disconnected from Leasing Reality

The facts:

  • NRI buying is RISING — NRI share of residential purchases at 20% (up 5 percentage points vs prior period), with NRI share within premium/luxury at 25%; remittances at $73 bn (12.8% YoY) provide the funding base.
  • Supply is skewed premium: 34% of new launches in the ₹80 lakh–₹1.5 cr band and 24% in the ₹1.5–2.5 cr band — developers are chasing NRI and affluent buyers, not mass-market demand.
  • Retail mall vacancy at 4.8% vacant (7th percentile) is near a decade low — physical retail is tighter than at any point in the post-COVID recovery.
  • But Embassy REIT at ₹370/unit, Mindspace at ₹345/unit, and Brookfield at ₹263/unit are all down sharply from prior prints — REIT investors are pricing office risk that leasing data (21 mn sq ft / quarter absorption) does not yet justify.
  • Nifty 50 at 22,422 has fallen five consecutive sessions; India VIX rising to 14.46 (from 10.35 five months ago) confirms the anxiety is structural, not a single-day event.

The tension: Physical retail is at a decade-low vacancy while office REITs trade as if the leasing cycle is turning — the divergence between asset classes within real estate is as wide as it has been in this cycle.

Bottom line: Overweight physical retail exposure (mall operators, retail-anchored developers) over office REITs until Nifty IT — falling five sessions straight to 22,422 — stabilises and GCC demand visibility improves.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Four Risks, One Trigger — Fuel and the Carry Unwind

The facts:

  • Fuel inflation at 22.93% YoY (95th percentile) — if Brent sustains above $100/bbl, every $10 adds ~$15 bn to India's annual import bill, widening CAD by ~40 bps and pressuring INR at 96.15 (91st percentile) toward 98.
  • BOJ rate at 1.0% p.a. (86th percentile) — a further hike compresses the yen carry trade, triggering EM outflows; FII flows already showed two months of net selling before this month's ₹29,631 cr net / month reversal.
  • CD ratio at 81.4% credit ÷ deposits (75th percentile) with corporate bond issuance at a decade-low ₹18,700 cr / month — if deposit growth stays at 10.8% YoY while credit runs at 17.1% YoY, NBFC funding costs rise and the weakest borrowers reprice first.
  • Nifty 50 falling five consecutive sessions to 22,422 with India VIX at 14.46 — a break below 22,000 would trigger passive outflows and test DII absorption capacity at current SIP run-rates.

The tension: Fuel inflation and the BOJ are external risks that RBI cannot offset with domestic tools; the CD ratio and equity correction are domestic risks that compound if the external shocks materialise simultaneously.

Bottom line: Watch Brent crude this week: a close above $105/bbl makes the RBI's 5.1% YoY inflation forecast untenable and forces a hawkish pivot that the equity market is not priced for.

  • Launch mix (supply): 35% of new launches priced above ₹1.5 cr; affordable just 14% — sales split by band not published this print
  • City momentum: Hyderabad leads at 15% sales YoY; Chennai trails at -10%

The tension: NRI demand is rising and developers are launching 35% of new supply above ₹1.5 cr — both sides are crowding into the top end, and whether sales absorb that supply is the number this print does not publish.
