India Macro Regime

Services Carry the Headline — Goods Economy Quietly Decelerates

The facts:

  • PMI Composite at 54.6 (83rd percentile) is services-driven: PMI Services 54.5 vs PMI Manufacturing 52.8, the widest gap in four months.
  • IIP overall slowed to 6.7% YoY from 8.8% YoY; core sector decelerated to 5.4% YoY from 6.0% — goods-economy momentum is fading.
  • But IIP Capital Goods at 16.1% YoY and capacity utilisation rising to 77.4% of capacity (74th percentile) confirm the investment cycle is intact.
  • GST at ₹1,99,853 cr / month — up from ₹1,74,116 cr / month — is a 15% single-month jump that warrants decomposition against e-way bill data before treating as a demand breakout.

The tension: The headline composite flatters: services buoyancy is masking goods deceleration, and a GST print of this magnitude in a single month historically reflects advance filings or formalization reclassification as much as genuine demand.

Bottom line: Watch PMI Manufacturing: a print below 52 would confirm goods-economy stall and force a downgrade to the consensus 7.8% YoY growth narrative.

Global Macro Regime

US Bond Market Tightening Where Central Banks Won't — EM Pays the Price

The facts:

  • US 10Y Treasury at 5.28% yield (96th percentile) — up from 5.24% — is embedding real tightening that the Fed funds rate at 3.75% p.a. (68th percentile) does not capture.
  • US GDP SAAR at 2.2% SAAR is recovering from 1.5%, but US Core PCE at 3.01% YoY (sticky, up from 2.98%) means the Fed cannot pivot — the bond market is doing the work.
  • BOJ at 1.0% p.a. (86th percentile, up from 0.75%) is unwinding the yen carry trade; Euro Stoxx 50 at a 100th-percentile 6,242 and Nikkei at a 100th-percentile 70,147 are priced for a world where this tightening does not transmit.
  • But China GDP at 4.3% YoY (down from 5.0%) with PMI Composite at 49.5 (contraction) removes the commodity-reflation escape valve for EM.

The tension: DM equity indices are at 100th-percentile valuations while the bond market is pricing a tightening cycle that has not ended — and China's demand contraction means commodity prices cannot bail out EM current accounts.

Bottom line: BOJ is the underappreciated tail: each 25bps hike unwinds carry trades that have funded EM positioning — watch the next BOJ meeting for the signal that forces India FII repatriation.

Liquidity Conditions

Domestic Flows at Record Highs — But the Funding Gap Underneath Is Widening

The facts:

  • SIP inflows at ₹32,297 cr / month (92nd percentile, rising two consecutive months) and DII equity net at ₹58,502 cr net / month (100th percentile) are providing a structural bid to Indian equities.
  • MF AUM at ₹87.08 lakh cr (86th percentile) and SIP AUM at ₹18.62 lakh cr (93rd percentile) confirm the retail financialisation trend is deepening.
  • But bank deposit growth at 10.8% YoY vs credit growth at 17.1% YoY — a 630bps divergence — means the same household savings flowing into MFs are not flowing into bank deposits, tightening the system's funding base.
  • FII equity net at ₹29,631 cr net / month (83rd percentile) looks strong but the recent sequence is -₹114 cr → -₹3,112 cr → +₹29,631 cr — one month of recovery after two of outflows, not a trend.

The tension: Record SIP and DII flows are masking a structural deposit shortfall: retail money is choosing mutual funds over bank deposits, which compresses the deposit base precisely as credit demand accelerates.

Bottom line: If deposit growth does not recover toward 13% YoY within two months, expect RBI macro-prudential action — risk-weight hikes on unsecured lending — before any rate move.

Equity + Real Estate Implications

NRI Demand and Premium Supply Diverge From Equity REIT Pricing

The facts:

  • NRI buying is RISING — NRI share of residential purchases at 20% (up 5 percentage points vs prior period), with NRI share within premium/luxury at 25%; remittances at $73 bn (12.8% YoY) provide the funding base.
  • Premium (₹80 lakh–₹1.5 cr) commands 34% of new launch supply and luxury (₹1.5–₹2.5 cr) a further 24% — developers are building for the top half of the market; affordable (14% of launches.
  • Hyderabad leads with sales +15% YoY and launches +120% YoY — a supply surge that, at 29 months of unsold inventory, risks price pressure; NCR sales at -1% YoY despite prices up 12% YoY signals affordability fatigue.
  • But office REITs are pricing the opposite signal: Embassy REIT at ₹370/unit (down from ₹429), Mindspace at ₹345/unit (down from ₹498), Brookfield at ₹263/unit (down from ₹330) — all down 14–31% from prior, while Bengaluru office rents rose to ₹100.6/sqft/mo from ₹94.

The tension: Physical office rents are rising and retail mall vacancy is at a 7th-percentile 4.8% vacant — yet listed REITs have de-rated 14–31%, suggesting the market is pricing US 10Y yield competition, not underlying asset fundamentals.

Bottom line: REIT de-rating is a US 10Y yield story, not an India real estate story — if US 10Y stabilises below 5.0% yield, Embassy and Mindspace offer a mean-reversion trade with fundamental support.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Four Risks, One Trigger — US Yields Sit Behind All of Them

The facts:

  • US 10Y at 5.28% yield (96th percentile, rising 3 of last 5 periods) — every 25bps further compresses India's rate differential, accelerates FII outflows, and pressures INR toward 97–98 ₹.
  • Fuel inflation at 22.93% YoY (95th percentile) with Brent at $100.47/bbl — a $10/bbl spike adds ~$15 bn to India's annual import bill, widens CAD by ~40bps, and feeds CPI with a two-quarter lag.
  • CD ratio at 81.4% credit ÷ deposits (75th percentile) with NBFC credit at 14.9% YoY — if deposit growth does not recover, RBI risk-weight action or a liquidity squeeze in NBFC wholesale funding is the next macro-prudential move.
  • Hyderabad residential launches at +120% YoY against 29 months of unsold inventory — a supply-demand mismatch that, if sales momentum reverses, could trigger developer stress in the city leading the national launch cycle.

The tension: All four risks have a common amplifier: a US 10Y yield that stays above 5.25% yield tightens India's external financing, pressures INR, raises import costs, and reduces RBI's room to ease — a feedback loop the base case does not price.

Bottom line: Watch US 10Y Treasury this week: a print above 5.35% yield would force RBI FX intervention, compress domestic liquidity, and make the CD ratio stress acute within 30 days.

NRI Bid Rising — Top-End Supply Building

The facts:

  • Launch mix (supply): 35% of new launches priced above ₹1.5 cr; affordable just 14% — sales split by band not published this print
  • City momentum: Hyderabad leads at 15% sales YoY; Chennai trails at -10%
  • NRI buying is RISING: 20% of residential purchases (+5 pp vs prior period in source); 25% within premium/luxury; concentrated in Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad
  • Office: Bengaluru leads leasing at 5.2 mn sq ft / quarter (11% YoY); GCCs take 42% of leasing

The tension: NRI demand is rising and developers are launching 35% of new supply above ₹1.5 cr — both sides are crowding into the top end, and whether sales absorb that supply is the number this print does not publish.

Bottom line: Watch the sales-share split by band next quarter: if luxury sales share prints below its 35% launch share, unsold inventory builds at the top end first, NRI bid or not.
