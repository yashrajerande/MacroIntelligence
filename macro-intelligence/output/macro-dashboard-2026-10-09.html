India Macro Regime

7.8% GDP, 6.7% IIP — Services Papering Over a Goods Slowdown

The facts:

  • PMI Composite at 54.6 (83rd percentile) holds up — but manufacturing PMI slipped to 52.8 from 53.5 while services PMI rose to 54.5 from 53.3: the divergence is widening.
  • IIP YoY slowed to 6.7% YoY from 8.8%; core sector decelerated to 5.4% YoY from 6.0% — goods output losing momentum even as capital goods at 16.1% YoY signals investment intent.
  • Two-wheeler sales at 19.7% YoY and PV sales at 16% YoY confirm rural and urban consumption are both alive — GST at ₹1,99,853 cr / month corroborates.
  • But capacity utilisation at 77.4% of capacity (74th percentile) is the ceiling: private capex will not accelerate meaningfully until this crosses 80%.

The tension: GDP at 7.8% YoY is a services-led number; the goods economy — IIP, core sector, manufacturing PMI — is decelerating in parallel. The headline is not wrong, but it is incomplete.

Bottom line: Watch manufacturing PMI: a print below 52 would confirm the goods-services split is widening into a structural drag, not a seasonal dip.

02
Global Macro Regime

US Resilience Exports Tightening — India Pays the INR Tax

The facts:

  • US PMI Composite at 56 (84th percentile) and US GDP SAAR at 2.2% SAAR — rising from 1.5% — give the Fed no reason to pivot; Fed Funds at 3.75% p.a. rose for the second consecutive month.
  • US 10Y Treasury at 5.28% yield (96th percentile) is doing the tightening the Fed hasn't — real economy stress is embedded in the curve, not the policy rate.
  • China GDP at 4.3% YoY (down from 5.0%) with PMI Composite at 49.5 (contraction) removes the commodity reflation backstop India's CAD needs.
  • But Euro Stoxx 50 at 6,127 (100th percentile) is still pricing a soft landing that the bond market — and China's PMI — are not confirming.

The tension: US growth resilience keeps the Fed on hold and 10Y yields elevated; China's deceleration prevents commodity prices from easing. India sits at the intersection of both pressures — import bill rising, carry attractiveness falling.

Bottom line: If US 10Y sustains above 5.30% yield, INR pressure intensifies and RBI's FX reserve deployment accelerates — watch the weekly reserve print.

03
Liquidity Conditions

Domestic Flows at Record Highs — But Funding the Wrong Side of the Trade

The facts:

  • DII equity net at ₹58,502 cr / month (100th percentile, rising two in a row) — domestic institutions are the marginal buyer as FII flows remain volatile: ₹29,631 cr / month this month after two months of net selling.
  • SIP inflows at ₹32,297 cr / month (92nd percentile) and SIP AUM at ₹18.62 lakh cr (93rd percentile) — retail participation is structurally deepening.
  • But bank deposit growth at 10.8% YoY vs credit growth at 17.1% YoY means the same household saving that funds SIPs is not flowing into bank deposits — the system's funding base is thinning.
  • NBFC credit growth accelerating to 14.9% YoY from 10.6% signals wholesale funding demand is rising precisely as the deposit pool tightens.

The tension: Equity liquidity is abundant — SIPs and DIIs are at record levels. Banking system liquidity is structurally stressed — the CD ratio at 81.4% credit ÷ deposits reflects a savings allocation shift that equity markets are celebrating and credit markets should fear.

Bottom line: Avoid leveraged NBFC exposure; the wholesale funding squeeze will show in Q3 NIM prints before it shows in NPAs.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

NRI Demand and Domestic SIPs Hold the Floor — REITs Signal the Ceiling

The facts:

  • NRI residential buying is RISING — NRI share at 20% of purchases (up from 15%, +5 pp), with 25% share within premium/luxury; INR at 97.09 makes dollar-denominated buyers 3% cheaper than six months ago.
  • Hyderabad leads city momentum: sales +15% YoY, launches +120% YoY, but 29 months of unsold inventory is a supply overhang that price momentum cannot sustain indefinitely.
  • Office REITs tell a different story — Embassy at ₹370/unit, Mindspace at ₹345/unit, Brookfield at ₹263/unit — all down sharply from prior levels as office absorption at 13.2 mn sq ft / quarter (6th percentile) disappoints.
  • But Nifty 50 at 22,232 (72nd percentile) with Bank Nifty at 54,515 (89th percentile) — equity valuations have not yet repriced the NIM compression that the CD ratio implies.

The tension: Residential real estate is supported by NRI flows and INR weakness; office REITs are being repriced lower as absorption data contradicts rent levels. Equities are pricing capex optimism while ignoring banking system funding stress.

Bottom line: Overweight residential developers with NRI-facing premium inventory in Mumbai and Bengaluru; underweight office REITs until absorption recovers above 16 mn sq ft / quarter.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Three Fuses, One Detonator — INR Is the Common Thread

The facts:

  • INR at 97.09 (94th percentile, weakening 4 of last 5 months) — Brent at $103.81/bbl means every further 2% INR depreciation adds ~₹8,000 cr / month to the import bill.
  • Fuel inflation at 22.93% YoY (95th percentile) — if Brent crosses $110/bbl, RBI's revised forecast of 5.1% YoY CPI becomes a floor, not a ceiling, eliminating any rate-cut optionality.
  • CD ratio at 81.4% credit ÷ deposits (75th percentile) with NBFC credit at 14.9% YoY — a macro-prudential risk-weight circular on unsecured or NBFC lending would reprice ₹15–20 lakh cr of credit overnight.
  • US 10Y at 5.28% yield (96th percentile) — a sustained move above 5.40% would trigger EM carry unwind; India's $680 bn FX reserves buffer is large but not infinite against coordinated outflows.

The tension: Each risk is individually manageable; the danger is sequencing — INR weakness feeds fuel inflation, which locks out rate cuts, which keeps real rates low, which accelerates deposit-to-equity migration, which tightens bank funding further.

Bottom line: Watch Friday's RBI weekly FX reserve data: a drawdown exceeding $3 bn in a single week signals active INR defence and raises the probability of emergency macro-prudential action.

NRI Bid Rising — Top End Supplied

The facts:

  • Affordable (< ₹40 lakh) took 16% of sales vs 14% of launches — supply and demand matched
  • City momentum: Hyderabad leads at 15% sales YoY; Chennai trails at -10%
  • NRI buying is RISING: 20% of residential purchases (+5 pp vs prior period in source); 25% within premium/luxury; concentrated in Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad
  • Office: Hyderabad leads leasing at 5.3 mn sq ft / quarter (47% YoY); GCCs take 41% of leasing

The tension: Supply is following demand into the same bands with a lag; the balance shifts one quarter after the sales mix does.

Bottom line: Track the launch share of the undersupplied bands next quarter — supply catching up is what turns a price story into an inventory story.
