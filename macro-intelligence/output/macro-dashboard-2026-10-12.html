India Macro Regime

Services Carrying Growth That Goods Cannot Confirm

The facts:

  • PMI Composite at 54.6 — 83rd percentile — is services-led: PMI Services rose to 54.5 while PMI Manufacturing slipped to 52.8.
  • IIP YoY decelerated to 6.7% YoY from 8.8%; core sector eased to 5.4% YoY from 6.0% — goods-economy momentum is fading.
  • But IIP Capital Goods at 16.1% YoY and capacity utilisation at 77.4% of capacity (74th percentile, up from 75.6%) signal the investment cycle is intact.
  • Two-wheeler sales at 19.7% YoY and PV sales at 16% YoY confirm broad consumption demand — but fuel inflation at 22.93% YoY is a deferred tax on both.

The tension: GDP at 7.8% YoY is services-driven and investment-supported, but the goods economy — IIP, core sector — is decelerating. A growth number that depends on services PMI and capex IIP while manufacturing softens is narrower than the headline implies.

Bottom line: Watch two-wheeler sales as the rural demand tripwire — a print below 12% YoY would signal fuel inflation has begun extracting its toll on mass-market consumption.

02
Global Macro Regime

DM Surveys Signal Recovery; Hard Data Says Otherwise

The facts:

  • US PMI Composite at 56 (84th percentile) and Euro Stoxx 50 at 6,173 (100th percentile) price re-acceleration.
  • But US GDP SAAR slowed to 2.2% SAAR from 2.5%; China GDP at 4.3% YoY from 5.0%, with PMI Composite at 49.5 — contraction territory.
  • US 10Y Treasury at 5.22% yield (95th percentile) is doing tightening work the Fed has not formally sanctioned; Fed Funds at 3.75% p.a. rising for two consecutive months.
  • BOJ at 1.0% p.a. (86th percentile) — yen carry unwind is the underappreciated transmission channel into EM liquidity.

The tension: Equity markets are pricing a DM growth reacceleration that nominal GDP data does not support. China's contraction-level PMI prevents commodity reflation from sustaining, while BOJ tightening is quietly unwinding the carry trades that funded EM inflows.

Bottom line: BOJ at 1.0% p.a. is the tail risk for India — each 25bps hike accelerates yen carry unwind and pressures FII equity flows within 60–90 days.

03
Liquidity Conditions

Domestic Flood, Foreign Trickle — and a Deposit Desert Beneath

The facts:

  • DII equity net flows at ₹58,502 cr net / month — 100th percentile, highest ever recorded — driven by SIP inflows at ₹32,297 cr / month (92nd percentile).
  • FII equity net at ₹29,631 cr net / month (83rd percentile) is positive on the surface — but FII debt flows are negative, and INR at 97.09 signals the aggregate foreign position is selling India.
  • But bank deposit growth at 10.8% YoY against credit growth of 17.1% YoY leaves a 630bps funding gap; corporate bond issuance at ₹18,700 cr / month sits at the 0th percentile — no capital market relief.
  • MF AUM at ₹87.08 lakh cr (86th percentile) is a structural positive, but SIP YoY growth has decelerated to 14% YoY from 16% — the marginal SIP buyer is slowing.

The tension: Equity liquidity is domestically abundant and at record levels, but the banking system's funding base is structurally thin. The two pools are not connected — SIP money does not solve the deposit gap, and the CD ratio at 81.4% will force the RBI's hand before equity flows do.

Bottom line: Watch the CD ratio — a print above 82.5% (prior level) would signal deposit mobilization has stalled and macro-prudential tightening via risk weights is imminent.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

NRI Bid Holds Premium Segment; Office REITs Price a GCC Retreat

The facts:

  • NRI buying is RISING — NRI share of residential purchases at 20% (up 5 pp vs prior period), with 25% share within premium/luxury; remittances at $140 bn (12.8% YoY) provide the funding base.
  • Premium (₹80 lakh–₹1.5 cr) commands 34% of launch supply; Hyderabad leads sales momentum at 15% YoY with launches surging 120% YoY — 29 months of unsold inventory is the counterweight.
  • Office absorption at 13.2 mn sq ft / quarter (6th percentile) has fallen 37% in two quarters; GCCs at 41% of occupier mix are the swing factor — NCR absorbed 4.4 mn sq ft / quarter (+106% YoY) while MMR fell 20% YoY.
  • But Embassy REIT at ₹370/unit, Mindspace at ₹345/unit, and Brookfield at ₹263/unit are all down sharply from prior — the market has already repriced the absorption collapse.

The tension: NRI demand and remittance strength are sustaining premium residential, but office REITs are pricing a GCC pullback that the absorption data confirms. Bengaluru rents at ₹163/sqft/mo (95th percentile) rising while absorption falls is a landlord-occupier standoff — one side will blink.

Bottom line: Avoid office REITs until GCC absorption stabilises above 16 mn sq ft / quarter; overweight premium residential developers with NRI-facing inventory in Mumbai and Bengaluru.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Four Fuses, One Detonator — Crude Is Holding the Match

The facts:

  • Fuel inflation at 22.93% YoY (95th percentile) with Brent rising five consecutive months to $103/bbl — every $10 adds ~$15 bn to India's import bill and widens CAD by ~40 bps.
  • CD ratio at 81.4% credit ÷ deposits (75th percentile) with corporate bond issuance at a 0th-percentile ₹18,700 cr / month — NBFC funding stress is the most likely first casualty.
  • INR at 97.09 (94th percentile), weakening 4 of last 5 months — BOJ at 1.0% p.a. accelerating yen carry unwind is the external amplifier.
  • Hyderabad residential launches at 120% YoY against 29 months of unsold inventory — a supply-demand mismatch that a demand slowdown would convert into a price correction.

The tension: Crude is the common thread: it widens CAD, pressures INR, feeds CPI, and constrains RBI from cutting — all simultaneously. The other risks are manageable in isolation; crude above $110/bbl makes them simultaneous.

Bottom line: Watch Brent daily — a sustained print above $110/bbl triggers the CAD-INR-CPI feedback loop that forces RBI off its pause and reprices every rate-sensitive asset in the portfolio.

NRI Bid Rising — Top End Supplied

The facts:

  • Affordable (< ₹40 lakh) took 16% of sales vs 14% of launches — supply and demand matched
  • City momentum: Hyderabad leads at 15% sales YoY; Chennai trails at -10%
  • NRI buying is RISING: 20% of residential purchases (+5 pp vs prior period in source); 25% within premium/luxury; concentrated in Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad
  • Office: Hyderabad leads leasing at 5.3 mn sq ft / quarter (47% YoY); GCCs take 41% of leasing

The tension: Supply is following demand into the same bands with a lag; the balance shifts one quarter after the sales mix does.

Bottom line: Track the launch share of the undersupplied bands next quarter — supply catching up is what turns a price story into an inventory story.
