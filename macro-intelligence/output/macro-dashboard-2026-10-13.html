Services Carry Growth — Mass Consumer Quietly Steps Back

Bottom line: Watch two-wheeler sales as the mass-market proxy — a deceleration below 15% YoY would confirm the bifurcation is widening, not narrowing.

US Resilience Exports Tightening — China Cannot Offset

Bottom line: BOJ normalisation is the underpriced tail risk for India liquidity — watch the yen cross; a move through 140 ¥/$ accelerates EM carry unwinds.

Liquidity Conditions

Domestic Flood, Structural Drought — SIPs vs Deposit Gap

The facts:

  • SIP inflows at ₹32,297 cr / month (92nd percentile) — rising for two consecutive months; SIP AUM at ₹18.62 lakh cr (93rd percentile) growing 2.3% MoM vs accounts growing 1.1% MoM, signalling ticket-size inflation as affluent retail enters.
  • DII equity net at ₹58,502 cr net / month (100th percentile) — domestic institutions in full accumulation; FII equity net at ₹29,631 cr net / month positive but volatile (prior two prints: -₹114 cr, -₹3,112 cr net / month).
  • But deposit growth at 10.8% YoY against credit at 17.1% YoY leaves the banking system structurally short of stable funding.
  • Corporate bond issuance at ₹18,700 cr / month (0th percentile) — capital markets are not absorbing the overflow from the banking system.

The tension: Equity liquidity is abundant — SIPs and DIIs are at multi-year highs. Banking system liquidity is structurally stressed — the CD ratio at 81.4% and bond market at a 10-year low in issuance means credit growth is being funded by increasingly thin deposit margins.

Bottom line: If deposit growth does not accelerate to close the gap with credit within two quarters, watch for RBI macro-prudential action on risk weights — the playbook from FY23 unsecured lending is the template.

Equity + Real Estate Implications

Retail Tightest in a Decade; Office Oscillates — REITs Mispriced

The facts:

  • NRI buying is RISING — NRI share of residential purchases at 20% (up 5 pp vs prior period); NRI share within premium/luxury at 25%; remittances at $140 bn (12.8% YoY) provide the funding base.
  • Retail mall vacancy at 4.8% vacant (7th percentile, tightest in 10 years) — Hyderabad and Bengaluru lead residential sales at 15% YoY and 12% YoY respectively; NCR sales turned negative at -1% YoY.
  • Office absorption at 14.1 mn sq ft / quarter (7th percentile) masks violent oscillation — the prior five prints: 21 → 13.2 → 21 → 13.2 → 4.02 mn sq ft / quarter; GCC now 41% of occupier mix, displacing IT services at 15%.
  • But Embassy REIT at ₹370/unit, Mindspace at ₹345/unit, and Brookfield at ₹263/unit are all down sharply from prior levels — the market is pricing office uncertainty that retail vacancy data does not support.

The tension: Retail real estate is at a structural vacancy low; office absorption is oscillating at a structural low. REITs — which are predominantly office — are being sold as if both are weak. The divergence between retail tightness and office volatility is not yet reflected in REIT pricing.

Bottom line: Retail-heavy REIT exposure is underpriced relative to vacancy fundamentals; avoid pure-play office REITs until GCC demand stabilises above 14 mn sq ft / quarter for two consecutive prints.

Key Risks to Monitor

Four Fuses, One Spark — Ranked by Probability × Impact

The facts:

  • Fuel inflation at 22.93% YoY (95th percentile) with Brent at $105/bbl — every $10 adds ~$15 bn to India's import bill, widens CAD ~40 bps, and feeds back into CPI with a 2-quarter lag; RBI's own forecast already at 5.1% YoY.
  • CD ratio at 81.4% credit ÷ deposits (75th percentile) with corporate bond issuance at a 10-year low — if deposit growth does not recover, NBFC funding costs rise and the credit upcycle stalls before private capex confirms.
  • INR at 96.15 ₹ (91st percentile) with US 10Y at 5.22% yield — the carry differential is insufficient; FII equity flows reversed twice in the last three months and a third reversal would pressure RBI reserves.
  • BOJ at 1.0% p.a. (86th percentile) — yen carry unwind is the least-priced global tail risk; prior BOJ moves at this percentile have transmitted to EM within 60–90 days.

The tension: All four risks are connected: oil widens CAD, CAD pressures INR, INR weakness amplifies oil's rupee cost, and a BOJ shock could trigger the FII reversal that turns a managed depreciation into a disorderly one.

Bottom line: Watch RBI's inflation forecast at the next policy meeting — if it is revised above 5.5% YoY, the rate-cut narrative collapses and Bank Nifty at the 90th percentile becomes the most exposed index.

NRI Bid Rising — Top End Supplied

The facts:

  • Affordable (< ₹40 lakh) took 16% of sales vs 14% of launches — supply and demand matched
  • City momentum: Hyderabad leads at 15% sales YoY; Chennai trails at -10%
  • NRI buying is RISING: 20% of residential purchases (+5 pp vs prior period in source); 25% within premium/luxury; concentrated in Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad
  • Office: Hyderabad leads leasing at 5.3 mn sq ft / quarter (47% YoY); GCCs take 41% of leasing

The tension: Supply is following demand into the same bands with a lag; the balance shifts one quarter after the sales mix does.

Bottom line: Track the launch share of the undersupplied bands next quarter — supply catching up is what turns a price story into an inventory story.
