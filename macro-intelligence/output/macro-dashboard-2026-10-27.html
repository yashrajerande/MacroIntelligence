India Macro Regime

7.8% GDP, But the Goods Economy Is Losing Altitude

The facts:

  • India GDP at 7.8% YoY (64th percentile) — the RBI's own forecast is 6.7% YoY, a 110bps gap that implies statistical discrepancy, not consensus.
  • PMI Composite at 55.9 (85th percentile) and IIP at 8.0% YoY confirm services and manufacturing momentum.
  • But core sector at 4.8% YoY (50th percentile) is decelerating — the eight infrastructure industries that proxy physical goods movement are not keeping pace.
  • Two-wheeler sales at 33.1% YoY signal rural demand recovery; passenger vehicle sales at 7.9% YoY are falling for the second consecutive month — the economy's two halves are diverging.

The tension: Headline GDP and PMI signal broad expansion; core sector deceleration and PV sales slowdown suggest the goods economy is plateauing while services carry the aggregate number.

Bottom line: Watch core sector YoY: a print below 4.0% YoY would confirm goods-economy stall and challenge the GDP-PMI consensus.

Global Macro Regime

DM Equity Euphoria Meets Bond Market Reality

The facts:

  • Euro Stoxx 50 at 6,116 (100th percentile) and S&P 500 at 7,677 (100th percentile) — both at 10-year highs, pricing re-acceleration.
  • But US 10Y Treasury at 5.0% yield (90th percentile) is doing the Fed's tightening work; US GDP SAAR at 2.2% SAAR is decelerating from 2.5% SAAR.
  • BOJ rate at 1.25% p.a. (100th percentile), rising for the second consecutive month — yen carry unwind is not a tail risk, it is in progress.
  • Global PMI Composite at 54.3 (84th percentile) and US PMI at 58.4 (91st percentile) — survey data and bond markets are telling opposite stories.

The tension: Equity markets are pricing a growth re-acceleration that nominal GDP data does not support; bond markets at 5.0% yield are pricing persistent tightening that equity multiples have not absorbed.

Bottom line: BOJ at 1.25% p.a. is the underpriced tail: each 25bps hike unwinds carry trades that have funded EM equity inflows, including India's DII buffer.

Liquidity Conditions

SIP Autopilot Holds, But Institutional Conviction Has Thinned

The facts:

  • SIP inflows at ₹32,490 cr / month (93rd percentile), rising for three consecutive months — retail allocation is systematic, not sentiment-driven.
  • But SIP accounts at 10.06 mn (2nd percentile), down from 10.45 mn — fewer accounts generating higher inflows means ticket size is inflating, not participation broadening.
  • DII equity net at ₹51,829 cr / month (95th percentile) absorbing FII equity outflows of ₹49,935 cr / month (17th percentile) — the offset is near-perfect but fragile.
  • NFO collections at ₹998 cr / month (2nd percentile), down from ₹3,051 cr / month — fund houses are not launching because institutional demand for new product is absent.

The tension: Retail SIP flows are mechanically strong, but the market is being held up by a shrinking base of high-ticket investors; any SIP slowdown removes the only structural buyer absorbing FII exits.

Bottom line: Watch SIP account count: a further decline below 9.8 mn would signal retail participation is narrowing, not deepening — the DII buffer becomes less reliable.

Equity + Real Estate Implications

India VIX Rising Into Equity Highs — REITs Price the Office Risk

The facts:

  • NRI buying is RISING — NRI share of residential purchases at 18% (up 8 pp), with 25% share within premium/luxury; Bengaluru, Mumbai, and Hyderabad are the primary destinations.
  • Hyderabad leads city momentum: sales +15% YoY, launches +120% YoY, but unsold inventory at 28 months — the launch surge is running ahead of absorption.
  • India VIX at 17.16 (13th percentile historically, but rising for four of the last five sessions) while Nifty 50 at 22,753 — volatility is cheap relative to the macro risk stack.
  • But Embassy REIT at ₹370/unit, Mindspace at ₹345/unit, Brookfield at ₹263/unit — all down sharply from prior levels, pricing office absorption risk that GCC occupancy data (42% of leasing) does not yet justify.

The tension: NRI demand and DII flows are supporting residential and equity prices; REIT valuations are diverging downward as office absorption volatility (ranging 2.6–24.6 mn sq ft / quarter) makes income visibility impossible to price.

Bottom line: REITs are a buy only if Q4 office absorption exceeds 20 mn sq ft / quarter — below that, the yield case collapses against a 5.0% US 10Y.

Key Risks to Monitor

Three Fuses, One Detonator — Fuel, Carry, and Credit

The facts:

  • Fuel inflation at 22.93% YoY (95th percentile) — Brent at $110/bbl means every $10 move adds ~$15bn to India's annual import bill and widens CAD by ~40bps; INR at 95.24 has no buffer.
  • BOJ at 1.25% p.a. (100th percentile) — carry unwind risk is live; FII equity outflows already at ₹49,935 cr / month, and DII absorption at 95th percentile cannot sustain indefinitely.
  • Bank credit at 19.4% YoY vs deposits at 17.8% YoY — if the 160bps divergence widens further, RBI macro-prudential action on risk weights is the most probable policy response, not a rate cut.
  • US CPI at 3.72% YoY, rising for three consecutive months — a Fed re-acceleration scenario would push US 10Y above 5.25% yield and reprice all EM risk assets simultaneously.

The tension: Each risk is individually manageable; the scenario where Brent holds above $110/bbl, BOJ hikes again, and the Fed delays cuts simultaneously is not priced — India's $680bn reserve buffer buys months, not quarters.

Bottom line: Watch Brent crude this week: a close above $115/bbl triggers the CAD-INR-inflation feedback loop that forces the RBI's hand before the next MPC meeting.

Inflation Cycle
CPI 5.54% YoY; Fuel 22.93% YoY

The inversion: if core is benign and the RBI cuts on that basis, the second-order effect is that fuel and food inflation — both supply-side and import-driven — feed into rural wage expectations and WPI at 9.92% YoY feeds back into manufactured goods prices within two quarters. The RBI's own inflation forecast of 5.1% YoY already exceeds its 4% target midpoint; a cut here would be a policy error dressed as data-dependence.

Credit Cycle
Credit 19.4% YoY; CD ratio 81%

The last time India sustained a CD ratio above 80% while the repo rate was being held was the pre-taper tantrum period of FY13-14; within 12 months, emergency rate action followed. Today's setup differs in one critical respect: NBFC credit is accelerating at 15.8% YoY, services credit at 24.3% YoY, and vehicle credit at 19.7% YoY — the credit expansion is broad-based, not concentrated. That breadth makes macro-prudential tightening via risk weights more likely than a rate move.

NRI Bid Rising — Top End Supplied

The facts:

  • Affordable (< ₹40 lakh) took 16% of sales vs 14% of launches — supply and demand matched
  • City momentum: Hyderabad leads at 15% sales YoY (prices 6% YoY); Chennai trails at -10%
  • NRI buying is RISING: 18% of residential purchases (+8 pp vs prior period in source); 25% within premium/luxury; concentrated in Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi-NCR
  • Office: Bengaluru leads leasing at 5.2 mn sq ft / quarter (11% YoY); GCCs take 42% of leasing

The tension: Supply is following demand into the same bands with a lag; the balance shifts one quarter after the sales mix does.

Bottom line: Track the launch share of the undersupplied bands next quarter — supply catching up is what turns a price story into an inventory story.
