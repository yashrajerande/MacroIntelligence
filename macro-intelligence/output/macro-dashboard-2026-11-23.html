India Macro Regime

Services Carry the Headline — Goods Economy Lags Behind

The facts:

  • GDP at 7.8% YoY and PMI Composite at 56.5 signal broad expansion, with manufacturing (55.7) and services (55.8) both above 50.
  • IIP Capital Goods at 16.9% YoY and capacity utilisation at 77.4% of capacity — both rising two months running — confirm the investment cycle is real.
  • But core sector growth decelerated to 4.8% YoY (from 5.0% prior month), and GST at ₹2,31,290 cr / month includes a 23% month-on-month jump that may reflect advance filings, not pure demand.
  • Household debt at 47.8% of GDP (up from 45.5%) signals consumption is increasingly debt-funded — a structural fragility beneath the headline.

The tension: The investment cycle (capex goods, utilisation) and services PMI are genuine; the goods economy and informal sector are not keeping pace, and the GST record needs triangulation against e-way bill volumes before it earns its headline.

Bottom line: Watch core sector growth: a second consecutive month below 5.0% YoY would confirm the goods economy is decoupling from the investment headline.

02
Global Macro Regime

US Re-Accelerates, BOJ Shocks — EM Carry Trade in the Crossfire

The facts:

  • US GDP at 3.4% SAAR (rising three months: 0.7%→1.5%→2.2%→3.4%) with US PMI Composite at 58.4 — the strongest DM growth print in this cycle.
  • But US CPI at 3.72% YoY (rising three months straight) and Core PCE at 3.12% YoY mean the Fed at 3.88% p.a. is not done — the market is underpricing the terminal rate.
  • BOJ at 1.25% p.a. — a 100th-percentile shock hike — is unwinding yen carry trades that funded EM positioning; US VIX at 35.89 (up from 19.3 five months ago) is the stress gauge.
  • Euro Stoxx 50 at 5,942 has fallen five sessions in a row; S&P 500 at 6,910 has fallen five sessions in a row — DM equity is repricing the rate path, not celebrating the growth.

The tension: US growth is re-accelerating on hard data, but equity markets are falling — because the bond market (US 10Y at 5.04% yield, 91st percentile) is tightening financial conditions faster than the growth data can justify optimism.

Bottom line: BOJ is the underappreciated tail risk: each 25 bps hike unwinds carry trades that have funded India FII positioning — watch the next BOJ meeting for a second consecutive hike.

03
Liquidity Conditions

SIP Autopilot Holds — But Institutional Conviction Is Fading

The facts:

  • SIP inflows at ₹32,160 cr / month (94th percentile) remain structurally elevated, but SIP YoY growth has decelerated sharply to 8.9% YoY from 19.85% five months ago.
  • Equity MF net inflows fell to ₹32,994 cr / month from ₹38,307 cr / month prior month — a ₹5,313 cr / month drop in a single period.
  • FII equity net at -₹4,516 cr / month, reversed from +₹29,631 cr / month three months ago; DII net at ₹6,222 cr / month, down from ₹58,502 cr / month — both sides of the institutional bid are shrinking.
  • But MF AUM at ₹83.39 lakh cr (86th percentile) is holding, supported by mark-to-market on a Nifty that has risen four of the last five sessions.

The tension: Retail SIP flows are the floor, but the institutional bid — both FII and DII — has contracted sharply; if SIP growth continues decelerating toward single digits, the floor itself becomes a question.

Bottom line: Watch equity MF net inflows: a print below ₹28,000 cr / month would signal retail conviction is following institutional money out the door.

04
Equity + Real Estate Implications

Bank Nifty at All-Time High, REITs at Multi-Year Lows — Pricing Opposite Realities

The facts:

  • NRI buying is falling — NRI share of residential purchases at 18% (down 2 pp from prior print), with NRI share within premium/luxury at 32%; remittances at $155.1 bn (14.5% YoY) are holding but not translating into property purchases.
  • Hyderabad leads residential momentum: sales +15% YoY, launches +120% YoY, price +6% YoY, 28 months unsold inventory — the launch surge risks a supply overhang within 6 quarters.
  • Bank Nifty at 64,635 (100th percentile) is pricing a banking system with pristine funding; Embassy REIT at ₹370/unit, Mindspace at ₹345/unit, Brookfield at ₹263/unit — all down 12–31% from prior prints — are pricing office demand destruction.
  • Office absorption at 4.02 mn sq ft / quarter (0th percentile, down from 19.5 mn sq ft / quarter five months ago) with GCCs at 42% of occupier mix — if GCC leasing has paused, the REIT income thesis breaks.

The tension: Bank Nifty at an all-time high assumes credit growth sustains without funding stress; REIT prices at multi-year lows assume office absorption has structurally broken. Both cannot be right simultaneously — one is mispriced.

Bottom line: Avoid office REITs until Q4 2026 absorption data confirms whether the 79% quarterly collapse is a one-quarter anomaly or a GCC structural pullback.

05
Key Risks to Monitor

Three Fuses, One Detonator — Inflation, Carry, and Credit

The facts:

  • Fuel inflation at 22.93% YoY (95th percentile, up 288 bps in one month) with Brent at $89.7/bbl flat — any crude spike above $95/bbl pushes headline CPI to 6.5%+ YoY and forces RBI's hand.
  • BOJ at 1.25% p.a. (100th percentile) unwinding carry trades: FII equity net already at -₹4,516 cr / month; a second BOJ hike could accelerate outflows and test INR at 94–95.
  • CD ratio at 82% (75th percentile) with services credit at 28.3% YoY — if deposit growth slips below 15% YoY, the regulator's macro-prudential toolkit (risk weight hikes on unsecured lending) gets activated within one quarter.
  • But S&P 500 falling five sessions in a row to 6,910 with US VIX at 35.89 — a global risk-off that deepens could compress India's equity risk premium and trigger passive EM outflows regardless of domestic fundamentals.

The tension: Each risk is individually manageable; the danger is sequencing — a BOJ hike triggers carry unwind, FII outflows weaken INR, imported fuel inflation accelerates, RBI is forced to hike into a credit system already running on thin deposit cover.

Bottom line: Watch India CPI (next release): a print above 6.3% YoY would make an RBI rate hike within two meetings near-certain and reprice the entire rate-sensitive complex.

NRI Bid Fading — Top End Supplied

The facts:

  • Premium (₹80 lakh – ₹1.5 cr) took 31% of sales vs 31% of launches — supply and demand matched
  • City momentum: Hyderabad leads at 15% sales YoY (prices 6% YoY); Chennai trails at -10%
  • NRI buying is FALLING: 18% of residential purchases (-2 pp vs earlier fetched print); 32% within premium/luxury; concentrated in Mumbai MMR, Bengaluru, Hyderabad
  • Office: Bengaluru leads leasing at 5.2 mn sq ft / quarter (11% YoY); GCCs take 42% of leasing

The tension: NRI share is falling while the top-end bands are adequately supplied — the price support the market has leaned on is thinning.

Bottom line: Watch luxury launch shares: if developers keep launching into the top end as NRI demand fades, unsold inventory in the ₹1.5 cr+ bands is the next print to turn.
